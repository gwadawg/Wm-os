## Framework 1: The Urgency Checkpoint
When to use: Between the Discovery phase and the Pitch phase — before you ever present your offer.

Purpose: To surface urgency objections before they become close-killers. If the prospect isn't "right now" ready, you do not move to the pitch.Step-by-Step Process

Step 1 — Label their situation After current situation + desired situation, summarize it back:

"You mentioned you're working on [X], and obviously with transitioning to [Y], you're really looking to get [Z]. But the roadblocks that have been blocking you are [A, B, C]. Does that make sense? Did I miss anything?"

## Step 2 — The Urgency Checkpoint One-Liner
"Well, if everything's kind of checking your boxes — obviously I still need to show you what we do — but if everything checks your boxes, the last thing I'm wondering is: what's your timeline on actually getting something started? With us, with anybody else — I'm just taking a temp check. Was it yesterday? Right now? Two weeks from now? A month? What does that actually look like for you?"

## Step 3 — Evaluate the answer
## Their Answer
## Your Move
### "Yesterday" / "Right now"
## Proceed to pitch
### "A couple weeks" / "Shopping around"
Do NOT move to pitch. Unpack it.

### Step 4 — If they delay, unpack it (not with resistance)
Do NOT say: "How has waiting helped you up to this point?" — that creates sales resistance.

### Instead, say:
"Absolutely, if that's the direction you want to go. I'm just curious — what makes you want to wait? You said you want to shop around a little. Is it more that you want to weigh out options to find the best price? Or is there some preloaded thought in your head about why you want to weigh all your options?"

## Step 5 — Use the analogy to reframe
"It's like saying, 'I want to date a bunch of different people, and if I find the right one, I'll fully commit.' But the truth is — relationships are built, not found. You'll always find someone more attractive. So what's the silver bullet you're actually looking for?"

### Step 6 — Isolate the silver bullet, then close the loop
"So if I could present that silver bullet to you today — exactly what you're looking for — you're telling me you'd still want to delay that? Why?"

Expected response: "I mean... if it was exactly what I'm looking for, I'd probably do it today."

Now you move to the pitch.

### Framework 2: The Temp Check (Pre-Price Drop)
When to use: At the end of your pitch, right before you drop the price.

Purpose: Objection prevention. Re-confirm commitment before the number lands so you don't get a ghost or a brush-off after the price reveal.

## Step-by-Step Process
## Step 1 — Keep them engaged during the pitch with micro tie-downs
### After each section of your pitch:
"Does that make sense? Any questions, comments, concerns, death threats on that?"

Don't just pitch and check in with a flat "does that make sense?" — make it conversational and keep them nodding along.

### Step 2 — The Temp Check (before price)
"Before we get into everybody's favorite part — I want to make sure all of this actually aligns with you and is what you're looking for. I know earlier you said you were two feet in if this was what you needed. So before we jump into the price — are you still feeling two feet in?"

Step 3 — Listen to their TONE, not just their wordsKey rule: If they give you the "yes" you want but the tone doesn't match — call it out.

## Step 4 — Call out the tonality directly
"You don't sound too confident. Be real with me — you're not going to hurt my feelings, I've got my big boy pants on. I want to make sure this is actually what you're looking for."

## Step 5 — Use the worst-case scenario close
"People who aren't two feet in and jump into this end up just wanting a refund. I don't want that to be the situation. I want to make sure we can actually move forward and get you what you need. So — is this what you're looking for?"

## Key Word Tracking Rules
Track commitment language — "two feet in," "right now," "yesterday" = green light. "Maybe," "I guess," "kind of" = red flag.

Track tonality over words — A "yes" with hesitation is a "no" in disguise.

Mirror their exact words back — If they said "two feet in," use that exact phrase in your temp check.

Never skip a checkpoint — Jumping to price without the temp check is how you get ghosted at the close.

Unpack, don't push — When there's resistance, get curious. Ask what the silver bullet is. Don't argue.

## Pre-Pitch
### Step 1: Acknowledge & Validate
Do not push back immediately. Lead with empathy and curiosity.

"Yeah, dude, that's completely fair. Thank you for being honest with me and bringing that up."

### Then open the door to understanding the root:
"I'm curious — what's the reason you don't make decisions the same day? Tell me a little bit more about that."

### What you're listening for:
## Past bad experiences with decisions
### A belief that "sleeping on it" = making better decisions
## A pattern of returning to comfort zone before committing
## Step 2: Identify the Root Need
### Once they share their reason, reflect it back and name the underlying need:
"So what I'm hearing is — you want to make sure you make the right decision. Is that right?"

They will say yes. Now you've anchored the conversation to their actual need: making good decisions, not avoiding decisions.

### Step 3: Validate Their Perspective (Given Their Experience)
### Acknowledge that their logic makes sense based on what they've been through:
"That makes total sense. Given the experiences you've had, the lesson you pulled — that sleeping on it and seeing if it still feels right from a place of comfort — that's a completely reasonable conclusion to draw."

This lowers defensiveness and builds trust before the reframe.

## Step 4: Ask Permission to Reframe
### Never force a new perspective. Ask for the door to open:
### "Would you be open if I shared another perspective on that?"
They say yes. Now you have full permission to coach.

## Step 5: Deliver the Reframe — The Comfort Zone Logic
Walk them through this sequence of agreed-upon truths, one at a time. Get a "yes" at each step before moving forward.

### Reframe Sequence (Word Tracking)
### Point 1 — Growth only happens outside the comfort zone:
"So oftentimes what happens is — the lesson you pulled from those past experiences might not be the full picture. When you go back to your status quo, back to your comfort zone, back to your routines, and then ask yourself if the decision still feels right from that place... do you think that's going to take you outside of your comfort zone where growth happens? Or keep you inside it?"

### (They agree: growth only happens outside the comfort zone.)
### Point 2 — Your current results come from your current decisions:
"Would you agree that all the results you have in your life right now are a direct result of the decisions you've made up to this point?"

### (They say yes.)
### Point 3 — A different life requires a different way of deciding:
"And would you agree that to get to a radically different life — one that's closer to your goals and free from the problems you're facing now — that's going to require a different way of making decisions?"

### (They say yes.)
### Point 4 — Close the loop:
"So then would it make sense that if you're trying to get to a place of growth you've never been before... that place is not going to be inside your comfort zone? And therefore, maybe what we need to try is a different way of making decisions — one where instead of going back to comfort to decide, you make the decision outside of your comfort zone, with accountability, in a way that's different than how you've decided before — so you can get a life that's different than what you've had before. Would you not agree?"

### Step 6: Handle Resistance (If They Say No)
### If they push back at any point in the sequence:
Do not argue. Return to the belief and work through it.

### Ask: "Help me understand — where does that not land for you?"
Re-anchor to their stated need: making the right decision.

Continue walking through the logic until they can see it.

## Step 7: Reinforce Your Role as the Leader
### Internally remind yourself — and externally model — this principle:
Your job on the call is to facilitate optimal decision-making. Taking 24–48 hours to decide from a place of comfort is not optimal. You are the leader in this conversation. Lead them to a better way.

Note: You must also make decisions this way yourself. You cannot coach someone to a standard you don't hold personally.

Deciding from inside your comfort zone will only ever produce more of the life they're not happy with. The goal is not to pressure them — it's to show them that the way they've been deciding is the very thing keeping them stuck.

They say the same words as the alpha — but the context is completely different. You do NOT reschedule and move on. You stay in it.

Step 1 — Don't Reschedule. Isolate First.

### Acknowledge briefly, then redirect immediately:
"Hey, before we start looking at times on the calendar and all that — I don't really feel like you're a 10 out of 10 on the process. Maybe I'm misreading this. Can you help me understand where you're at?"

What you're doing: Slowing the exit down without being pushy. You're making it about THEM, not about closing.

### Step 2 — Get a Number (1–10 Scale)
Force clarity. Vague feelings let people off the hook.

"On a scale of 1 to 10 — one being 'get off the phone, I hate this guy,' ten being 'I'd hand you my credit card right now if I had it' — where are you honestly at?"

### Listen for:
7–8 = there's a real objection underneath. Keep going.

9–10 = it's pure fear / habit. The reframe is easier.

Below 6 = there's a product fit or trust issue. Dig deeper before proceeding.

### Step 3 — Target a 9, Not a 10
### Remove the impossible standard immediately:
"Look — a 10 doesn't exist. Nobody can know the future, so let's just take that off the table entirely. But a 9 — how do we get you to a 9? What part of the process isn't making perfect sense to you? Because if you saw it the way I see it, we'd already be doing this. So help me understand what's holding you back."

Why this works: You're not asking them to be certain. You're asking them to get close enough. That's a much easier ask psychologically.

## Step 4 — Let Them Name the Fear
### Stay quiet. Let them talk. The most common answer will be some version of:
### "It sounds good in theory... but how do I know it's actually going to work?"
This is the real objection. Not "I don't decide on the spot." That was just the exit door they were heading for.

### Step 5 — Validate the Fear, Then Dismantle It
### First — agree with them:
"100%. And I'm glad you said that — because you don't know. It's literally not possible to know the future. I'm not going to sit here and tell you I can guarantee an exact result, because if I did that I'd be selling you snake oil."

### Then — reframe certainty with evidence:
"What I do know is that Facebook is a multi-billion dollar company that makes ALL of its money from advertisers — not users. The biggest companies in the world are dumping hundreds of millions into these ads not because they don't work, but because they continue to bring profit month after month."

### Then — normalize failure as part of the process:
"Part of running ads is that sometimes a perfectly qualified lead shows up and something goes wrong on the tech side. Part of it is a great ad brings someone in and the sales conversation doesn't land. That's part of doing business. If we're waiting for everything to be perfect — Tony Robbins says perfectionism is the lowest standard of all, because perfect doesn't exist. Perfectionism is just an excuse to do nothing."

## Step 6 — Paint the Cost of Waiting
Make inaction more painful than action. Use their own numbers against them.

"Dude — you told me your goal is $50k a month. That's almost $2,000 a day. Every single day you're sitting on the rocking chair having this conversation in your head, that's $2,000 slipping by. That's not a decision you need 24 hours to make — that's a conversation you can have in your head for 30 minutes after we hang up. And we both know what happens when you do that. You go to bed, wake up, grind for 12 hours, mow the lawn, and do it again. Nothing changes."

Key move: Use the exact goal number they gave you earlier in the call. It hits harder when it's their own words.

### Step 7 — Remove All the Risk (Trial Close)
Now that the fear is addressed and the cost of waiting is real — take all the risk off the table:

"Here's what I'm willing to do. If you're willing to step up today — I'll enroll you with a guarantee that in the next 72 hours, if you get in here and it's not what we said it was, you can walk. We tear up the contract, shake hands, still friends. No hard feelings."

"I'm not doing this because I want you to waste our team's time. I genuinely believe you're going to love it in here. But I want you to have an informed decision — and the only way to make a truly informed decision is by actually experiencing it."

Why this works: You've now eliminated the only logical reason to wait. There's no risk. The only thing left is their willingness to commit.

### Step 8 — Final Close (Get a Committed Yes)
"So if I can do all of that for you today — are you willing to step up and make it happen?"

### After they say yes — don't stop there. Get full buy-in:
"Do you really mean it? Are you actually going to give this everything you've got? Because if I go to my team and ask them to do this for you, and you don't show up fully — that reflects on me too. I've got a reputation to protect. So I need to know — are you really in?"

Why this works: A half-committed yes will lead to a refund or a ghost. This step locks in accountability and filters out the people who aren't truly ready.

## The Rule That Ties It All Together
The "I don't make decisions on the spot" objection from a struggling prospect is never about the decision. It's about fear dressed up as a personality trait. Your job is to strip the costume off, address the fear directly, remove the risk, and make inaction more expensive than action.

## Pricing Objection
### Sales Call Objection Breakdown & Word Tracks
Here's a full breakdown of every objection handled in this call, with word tracks you can practice.

### Objection 1: "I don't have that amount in liquid right now"
### What the prospect said:
"I do not have that amount right now in terms of liquid, so I wouldn't be able to pay it in full."

The Move: Reframe money as the easiest part — make commitment the real question.

### Word Track:
"A number is just a number. At the end of the day, that's the easiest part. What's really going to decide whether or not you can have success with this is whether you're committed or not. So are you committed to putting in the time, energy, and effort required to get the results — to make that [income goal], so you can [insert their personal why]? Are you committed to that?"

Why it works: He completely sidesteps the money objection and anchors the conversation to emotion and identity. Once the prospect says "yes" to commitment, the money conversation becomes a logistics problem, not a values problem.

### Objection 2: "The monthly payment of $900 isn't feasible — I'm laid off"
### What the prospect said:
"Honestly, right now I don't think it is. I'm laid off and I don't know how much I'll be getting from unemployment."

The Move: Validate, re-anchor to commitment, then introduce resourcefulness as the solution.

### Word Track:
"I hear you, man. The reason I'm giving you the option to break it down is because you said you're an 11 out of 10 committed. As long as that commitment is there, we can figure out how to get your foot in the door. So let's talk about getting resourceful. If we really need that extra bit of cash — dude, apply for DoorDash right now. It takes 60 seconds. You can literally do it while you're going through the material. You've got an Xbox? That's a distraction anyway — sell it. With the monthly payments, we'll get this paid off in a few months, and realistically, once you start making money through this, you'll pay off the rest with what you earn. Does that make sense?"

Why it works: He doesn't let the objection die there. He gives the prospect a concrete, immediate action (DoorDash, sell items) that makes the payment feel solvable today. He also future-paces — "you'll pay it off with the money you make."

### Objection 3 (Implied): "I don't know if I can make this work financially"
This one was never stated directly but was lingering. He handled it proactively.

The Move: Future-pace the ROI so the investment feels like it pays for itself.

### Word Track:
"If we really do commit to learning all the material, we can start making money with our brand in the next couple of months. So realistically, we'll just pay off the rest with the money we make through this."

Why it works: He removes the fear of the payment by showing the payment becomes irrelevant once results kick in.

## Certainty Reframe
For when the LO is hesitating, "needs to think about it," or just won't pull the trigger

Look, I get it. And I want to be honest with you about something.

I truly believe that this need for certainty is the number one reason most loan officers never build the business they're capable of. Because here's the thing — certainty doesn't exist. We don't know if we're going to get to the end of this call. We don't know if we're going to wake up tomorrow morning. Nothing in life is certain except death and taxes.

And because that's true, most loan officers are filled with great ideas, real ambition, real skill — but they never pull the trigger on anything. Because they want to know the future before it comes. This weird need for a crystal ball. And the crazy part? By doing nothing, you ARE certain — certain to get exactly what you've got right now.

The only way to get a result you've never had is to try something you've never done.

And isn't it true that every loan officer who's built something exceptional — who's dominating their market, closing deals on demand, not waiting on referrals — also took an exceptional risk to get there?

[NAME], in the last six months — when did you actually take a real risk on yourself? Like a real one, where you could have lost money if it didn't go well?

### [They say never.]
There's your answer right there. You're doing the exact opposite of what the most successful people in this space are doing.

And look — I'm not in the business of turning hesitators into action takers. But I genuinely don't think you're a hesitator. I think nobody ever just said this to you before. You've probably gotten a hundred pitches just like this, said "I need to think about it," got right to the edge of a real breakthrough — and talked yourself off the ledge.

You're like the kid on the high dive. He climbs up, starts bouncing, feels the fear, looks around — and then asks the lifeguard if he can come back down. And that kid? He's still on the ground. He's 45 years old, never jumped.

But if he just had two seconds of courage and jumped — he would've realized it wasn't that bad.

The best people in this industry — the ones doing what you told me you want to do — they're consistently making calculated bets where the upside is asymmetric to the downside. That's the game.
