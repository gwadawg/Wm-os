# Prospect Fallacies Framework

> **North star:** Name the **thinking pattern** behind a deferral or objection — then route to the [Conceptual Beliefs Framework](conceptual-beliefs-framework.md) and product objection docs. This is a **recognition layer**, not a manipulation playbook.

Universal reference for Waiz sales training and client LO education. Product-specific lines live in playbooks — do not duplicate word tracks here.

---

## Purpose

Give setters, closers, and LO coaches a shared vocabulary for **prospect-side cognitive patterns** that sound logical but block action. Fallacy labels help reps stop treating polite deferrals as final answers and stop arguing on the surface.

This doc **complements** belief installation — it does not replace it.

---

## Scope

| Included | Excluded |
|----------|----------|
| High-level fallacy definitions | Full course videos or long analogies |
| Belief-gap mapping (→ seven beliefs) | Word tracks and scripts (→ objection playbooks) |
| Ethical response **principles** | Pressure tactics, misrepresenting the prospect's position |
| RM sprinkle examples | Waiz B2B-specific rebuttal tables |

---

## When to use

- **Training:** Sprinkle 1–2 patterns into belief or objection modules — not a standalone 60-minute lecture.
- **Live calls:** Recognize pattern → stay in discovery → Socratic question until *they* articulate the gap.
- **Objection handling:** Only after belief work — fallacy name is for **rep clarity**, rarely spoken aloud to the prospect.
- **Coaching / QA:** "That was if-when — Cost belief still open."

---

## Operating flow

```text
Surface words → Name pattern (rep-side) → Map to belief gap → Socratic discovery
→ Belief checked? → Pitch / next step OR objection doc word track
```

| Step | Rule |
|------|------|
| 1. Recognize | Deferrals and soft nos often mask a fallacy or fear — not a hard disqualify |
| 2. Map | Each pattern ties to 1–2 beliefs in the [seven-belief ladder](conceptual-beliefs-framework.md) |
| 3. Reframe | Ask — don't lecture. Never say "that's a fallacy" on a live borrower call |
| 4. Escalate | Unchecked belief after discovery → [Objection Handling Hub](objection-handling-hub.md) or product objections doc |

## Category A — Self-limiting patterns (recognize + reframe)

Patterns the **prospect uses on themselves**. Your job: help them see the loop — without shame or jargon.

### 1. If-when fallacy (conditional deferral)

**What it is:** They believe they must achieve condition X *before* they can accept help — even though condition X is often **why** they need help.

**Sounds like:**

| Generic | RM borrower examples |
|---------|---------------------|
| "I'll lose the first 10 lbs myself, then hire a trainer" | "Let me pay down some debt first, then call you back" |
| "I'll save $100, then pay the advisor" | "When rates come down…" / "After tax season…" |
| "Once things settle at work, I'll start" | "When I save up for the appraisal…" |
| | "After we talk to the kids / spouse…" *(Support gap — not always if-when)* |

**Belief gap:** **Doubt** (can solve alone / wrong timing) · **Cost of inaction** (waiting feels safer)

**Response principle:**

- Do not argue the condition is wrong — explore **what happens while they wait**.
- Tie waiting to **their** stated pain ([Conceptual Beliefs §3](conceptual-beliefs-framework.md)).
- One Socratic line: *"What usually changes if you wait another 6–12 months on [their pain]?"*

**Do not:** Lecture "if-when fallacy" · Promise outcomes if they act now · Shame them for not starting sooner

---

### 2. Sunk cost fallacy (pot committed)

**What it is:** They continue a path because of **past** time, money, or emotional investment — even when the forward odds are poor.

**Sounds like:**

| Generic | RM borrower examples |
|---------|---------------------|
| "I'm already $160 in — might as go all in" | "We've already been in this mortgage / HELOC for years" |
| "We've been together 13 months — one more month" | "I don't want to go through another loan process after refi was denied" |
| "I already paid for that course — I have to finish" | "We already decided to sell in a few years — let's see how that goes" |
| "Markets are down — I have to wait for recovery" | "I've been dealing with this two years — I'll give it another year" |

**Belief gap:** **Cost of inaction** (status quo feels cheaper than change) · sometimes **Doubt** (change = admitting past choice failed)

**Response principle:**

- **Time > money** — waiting has a cost they won't get back (especially relevant for fixed-income / retiree ICP).
- Backward cost: *"What has waiting already cost you — money, stress, options?"*
- Forward cost: *"If nothing changes in the next 12–24 months, what gets harder?"*
- Validate dignity — never mock past decisions ([RM Doctrine §5](../../client-fulfillment/reverse-mortgage-dna/doctrine-rm-marketing.md)).

**Do not:** Pressure with doom · Guarantee that switching paths wins · Dismiss heir/spouse concerns as "sunk cost"

---

### 3. Status-quo bias

**What it is:** Preference for the current state because change feels risky, effortful, or identity-threatening — even when the status quo is painful.

**Overlap:** Often pairs with sunk cost and **Debt Frame** (RM: reverse feels like "last resort" debt, not retirement tool).

**Belief gap:** **Cost of inaction** · **Trust** (category skepticism)

**Response principle:** Calm urgency — compare cost of **staying** vs cost of **acting**, using their numbers only. Education-first for RM (FHA, counseling, non-recourse facts).

---

### 4. Smoke screen (rational mask)

**What it is:** A logical-sounding objection hiding an emotional blocker — fear, shame, or identity protection.

**Sounds like:** "Just looking" · "Send me info" · "Not the right time" · vague money without numbers

**Belief gap:** Usually **Pain** or **Cost of inaction** unchecked — sometimes **Trust**

**Response principle:** Treat as **data**, not rejection. Stay in discovery per [Conceptual Beliefs — operating principles](conceptual-beliefs-framework.md#operating-principles).

## Category B — Persuasion patterns (ethical use only)

These are **real cognitive levers** — useful when aligned with truth and the prospect's interest. Waiz standard: **advisor frame**, not predatory framing.

### 5. Social proof cluster (authority · tradition · bandwagon)

**What it is:** People weight what respected figures, long-standing practice, or peer behavior suggest — sometimes beyond the evidence.

| Variant | Ethical use | Avoid |
|---------|-------------|-------|
| **Authority** | HUD/FHA facts, licensed counselor requirement, credible third-party education | Fake stats, unverified guru quotes |
| **Tradition** | "Regulated HECM program since…" · long-tenure **verified** client patterns | "Ancient tribes used…" · unprovable longevity claims |
| **Bandwagon** | "Many homeowners in your situation explore this when…" | Fabricated popularity · pressure to conform |

**Belief gap:** **Trust** · **Doubt**

**Response principle:** Substantiated proof only — especially on RM ([Compliance Guardrails](../../client-fulfillment/reverse-mortgage-dna/rm-compliance-guardrails.md)). Social proof **supports** belief installation; it does not replace Pain/Cost discovery.

---

### 6. First-step framing (progressive commitment)

**What it is:** People stall when shown the **entire** journey. One clear, low-friction next step builds momentum better than a 12-step map.

**Not:** Deliberately exaggerating that one yes equals full transformation ("slippery slope" as manipulation).

**Belief gap:** **Trust** (overwhelm kills Trust) · supports **Doubt** resolution

**Response principle:**

- One next step: joint call, counseling intro, document list, BAMFAM slot — not full process dump on call 1.
- RM multi-call reality: [RM Conceptual Beliefs — pitch transition gate](../../client-fulfillment/client-sales/playbook-rm-conceptual-beliefs.md#pitch-transition-gate).

---

### 7. Resourcefulness vs affordability (value clarity)

**What it is:** "Can't afford" often means **value not yet clear** — not literal inability — when Pain, Cost, and Desire are checked.

**Belief gap:** **Money** — but diagnose **Cost + Desire first** ([Conceptual Beliefs §7](conceptual-beliefs-framework.md)).

**Response principle:** [Ferrari / resourcefulness frame](../../client-fulfillment/client-sales/playbook-rm-conceptual-beliefs.md#the-ferrari-frame-resourcefulness) — deepen outcome vs friction using **their** validated pain. Let resourcefulness emerge ("I could ask my son") — do not accuse them of lacking it.

**Do not:** Strawman their position ("so you're saying you're not resourceful") · Invent urgency · Guarantee appraisal "pays for itself"

---

## What we do not teach

| Anti-pattern | Why |
|--------------|-----|
| Labeling fallacies on live calls | Sounds salesy; breaks Trust with RM seniors |
| Misrepresenting their stated objection | Strawman destroys advisor credibility |
| Stacking guru quotes / fake statistics | Off-brand; compliance risk |
| Using fear slippery slopes | Regulated product + dignity standard |
| Fallacy training **before** belief framework | Recognition without belief map = clever rebuttals that don't close |

Use in existing modules — **5–10 minutes max** per touchpoint.

| Host module | Sprinkle |
|-------------|----------|
| [Conceptual Beliefs Framework](conceptual-beliefs-framework.md) | After Cost of inaction — introduce if-when + sunk cost as labels |
| [RM Conceptual Beliefs Playbook](../../client-fulfillment/client-sales/playbook-rm-conceptual-beliefs.md) | When Cost/Money sections stall — link pattern name |

| Call coaching | QA tag: `fallacy:if-when` / `fallacy:sunk-cost` on belief worksheet |
