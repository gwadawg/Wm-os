## The one job this call has to do
### 📌  The One Job of This Call
Get the prospect to show up to the Demo Call.

That's it. Do not try to close. Do not try to overcome every objection. Save your best material for the demo.

## Call Overview
The Discovery Call is a short, structured intake conversation. You are not selling on this call — you are qualifying and warming. The goal is to understand enough about the prospect's situation to (1) confirm they're worth a full demo, and (2) make them want to show up.

### This call has four stages:
## Stage
## Purpose
### 1 — Hell (The Problem)
Surface the pain. Get them thinking about what's not working.

### 2 — Bridges (What They've Tried)
Understand their history. Listen for objections brewing.

### 3 — Heaven (The Goal)
Get clarity on where they want to be and why it matters.

### 4 — Fuel (Commitment & Urgency)
Lock in their timeline and surface the real driver behind it.

### ⭐  Why This Order Matters
Hell → Bridges → Heaven is a deliberate sequence. It moves the prospect from pain → frustration → desire.

When they articulate their own problem and their own goal, they're already half-sold before the Demo Call begins.

### Stage 1 — Hell (The Problem)
Open by surfacing the main problem they're facing. Your job here is to get them to say out loud what isn't working.

## What to Do
### Ask about the main problem they're facing right now
### Keep it surface level — you're opening the wound, not digging into it
## Get them to conceptualize the problem in their own words
## Listen more than you talk
## What Not to Do
## Do not go deep on root causes
## Do not offer solutions or position Waiz Media yet
### Do not let this stage drag — get the pain on the table and move
### 💡  Pro Tip
If a prospect struggles to articulate their problem, ask: 'What does your pipeline look like right now compared to where you want it to be?' That question almost always unlocks the real answer.

### Stage 2 — Bridges (What They've Tried)
Ask what they've already done to solve the problem. This stage gives you critical intelligence about their objection landscape — without triggering those objections on this call.

## What to Do
### Ask: 'What have you tried to do to solve this in the past?'
Listen carefully for frustration, skepticism, or red flags (e.g., 'We've worked with 10 agencies and they all sucked')

### Take notes on what you hear — save it for the Demo Call
## What Not to Do
## Do not go deep on reframes or objection handling here
### Do not try to defend agencies or explain why Waiz Media is different
### Do not get into a debate — you'll lose the call
### ⚠️  Why Light Touch on This Stage
You are not trying to close them on this call. You are trying to get them to the Demo.

If you start handling objections now, you burn your best ammunition before the demo even starts.

Note what you hear. Save the reframes, proof points, and positioning for where they'll land hardest — the Demo Call.

### Stage 3 — Heaven (The Goal)
After surfacing pain and understanding history, shift the conversation forward. Where do they want to be? Why does it actually matter to them?

## What to Do
### Ask: 'If this was solved, what does that look like for you?'
### Follow up: 'Why does that matter to you personally?'
Listen for personal motivators — the real emotional driver beneath the business goal

Take notes, especially on anything personal (e.g., college fund, retirement, time with family)

## What Not to Do
## Do not dig deep or turn this into a therapy session
Do not pitch. You are not there yet.

Do not skip this stage — personal motivators are your most powerful demo material

### 📌  Note on Personal Motivators
When a prospect says something like 'I need to pay for my daughter's college' or 'I'm trying to get off the referral hamster wheel by the end of the year' — write it down verbatim.

These aren't just nice-to-haves. They are the real levers. A skilled closer will use them on the Demo Call.

### Stage 4 — Fuel (Commitment Level & Urgency)
This is the close of the Discovery Call. Fuel locks in urgency, surfaces the prospect's timeline, and creates a natural bridge to next steps. Run it on every call.

## The Three Questions — In Order
### #
## Question
## 1
### "When are you looking to put something in place?"
## 2
### "Okay gotcha — why is that?"
## 3
### "Anything else you want to add before we start talking next steps?"
## What Each Question Is Doing
Question 1 — Establishes timeline. Gets them on record with a timeframe. If they're vague, that tells you something.

Question 2 — Uncovers the real driver behind the timeline. The 'why' is almost always a personal motivator. Capture it.

Question 3 — Clears the deck. Gives them a chance to surface anything left on their mind before you transition to booking the Demo.

### 📌  Why This Stage Is Called Fuel
You're not closing a deal here — you're lighting a fire under the prospect's own urgency.

When they articulate their own timeline and their own reason for it, they've committed to the problem being real and time-sensitive.

That commitment is what gets them to show up to the Demo.

### 💡  Pro Tip
If a prospect says 'I'm not really sure when' or 'whenever,' push gently on Question 2.

Ask: 'If you could wave a magic wand, when would you want this solved?' Most people have a real answer — they just haven't been asked.

## What to Capture on Every Call
After the call, your notes feed the Demo. The closer needs this information to run a sharp, personalized pitch. If you don't capture it, that intel is lost.
