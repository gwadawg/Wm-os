## Overview
Every objection a prospect raises falls into one of three categories. Misdiagnosing the category is one of the most costly mistakes a closer can make — you end up using the wrong reframe, burning rapport, and triggering a negative feedback loop that kills the deal.

This document defines each objection type, how to identify it, and how to handle it in the context of the Waiz Media sales process.

## 📌 NORTH STAR
The one job of objection handling is not to 'overcome' — it's to correctly diagnose and respond. Wrong reframe, wrong outcome. Always isolate the objection type before you respond.

## The Three Categories of Objections
Every objection is one of these three. They are not interchangeable — the handling method for each is distinct.

## Category 1 — Fear-Based Objections
## What It Is
A fear-based objection occurs when a prospect projects a worst-case scenario onto the decision. They become emotionally paralyzed by the perceived risk — not because the logic doesn't add up, but because the emotional downside feels too large.

Example: A prospect who has been burned by three agencies already isn't saying 'I can't see the value.' They're saying 'I'm terrified of being burned again.' That's fear, not logic.

### Why They're Hard to Spot
People don't admit they're scared. They use smokescreens — surface-level excuses that mask the real emotional driver underneath. Natural knee-jerk responses like 'I need to think about it' or 'now isn't the right time' are often fear-based objections in disguise.

## ⚠️ IMPORTANT
The prospect is more likely to lie than to be rude — especially if they like you. Expect smokescreens. Don't take the surface objection at face value. Isolate first.

## How to Identify It
## Objection feels irrational or disproportionate to the actual risk
### Prospect is vague or evasive — can't clearly explain why they're hesitating
### You've answered their logical questions but they're still not moving
### History of bad agency experiences, burnout, or failure in this area
Emotional tone — frustration, resignation, or hesitation comes through in their voice

## How to Handle It
Fear-based objections require risk reversals and asymmetric reframes. The goal is to shift the prospect's internal calculus — make the downside of NOT acting feel larger than the downside of acting.

The core framework: Asymmetric Upside vs. Downside Decision-Making.

### Walk them through both scenarios clearly:
### What happens if this works? (Upside — quantify it in deal outcomes)
What happens if it doesn't, and we make it right? (Downside — reduce perceived risk)

### What happens if they do nothing? (Status quo — the often-missed third option)
## 💡 PRO TIP
Fear-based objections are the easiest to close once isolated — because they're almost always irrational. The prospect wants to move forward. They just need you to stand up for them when they can't stand up for themselves. Confidence and certainty are your most powerful tools here.

## Category 2 — Uncertainty-Based Objections
## What It Is
Uncertainty-based objections happen when the prospect lacks confidence in one of three areas: themselves, you (the rep), or the product/company/process. Unlike fear-based objections, these are about gaps in belief — not emotional panic.

## The Three Sub-Types
## Sub-Type
## What It Sounds Like
## Uncertainty in Themselves
'It probably won't work for me.' / 'I've tried everything.' / 'I'm just unlucky.' Low self-esteem, feels like an outlier.

## Uncertainty in You
'I'm not sure you understand my situation.' / Vague skepticism about your competence or approach.

### Uncertainty in Product / Company / Process
'How does this actually work?' / 'I've heard of similar things.' / Unclear on mechanics — even if they trust you and themselves.

## ⚠️ WATCH FOR THIS
Process uncertainty is the most commonly missed sub-type. A prospect can fully trust you, love the company, and believe in themselves — but if the process isn't crystal clear, the analytical mind won't commit. Watch for it.

## How to Identify It

### They ask clarifying questions about HOW something works, not IF it works
## They seem unconvinced despite agreeing with your logic
## Prospect sounds uncertain in their voice when discussing specific topics
## How to Handle It
Use lighter reframes compared to fear-based objections. The goal is to fill the gap in belief — not overcome an emotional barrier.

### Category 3 — Logistical / Logic-Based Objections
## What It Is
Logistical objections are legitimate external constraints that prevent the sale from closing right now. They are not emotional. They are not irrational. They are real — and they require a completely different approach.

## 🚨 CRITICAL MISTAKE TO AVOID
CRITICAL: If you treat a logistical objection like a fear-based one, you will lose the deal. Imagine trying to reframe someone's emotions when their budget genuinely resets in 60 days. Wrong tool, wrong outcome. Isolate first — always.

## The Three Sub-Types
## Sub-Type
### Definition & Example
## True Time
The timing is genuinely wrong — a baby was just born, it's the wrong season, or they're mid-transition. Not avoidance. Not fear. Actual timing.

## Logistical Money
They want the solution and believe in it — but they physically cannot access the funds right now. Budget resets, cash flow gaps, credit limits. This is different from being unqualified.

## Authority
The decision-maker is someone else. A business partner, a boss, a compliance officer. The prospect physically cannot move forward without approval from a third party.

## Important Distinction — Money Objections
There is a critical difference between a logistical money objection and an unqualified prospect:

Logistical money: They WANT it, BELIEVE in it, but CAN'T access funds right now. This is a scheduling problem, not a conviction problem.

Unqualified: They could find the money if they truly believed. If certainty were 100%, they'd make it happen. That's actually an uncertainty-based objection, not a logistical one.

Do not confuse the two. Misclassifying an unqualified prospect as logistically constrained wastes time and delays the right diagnosis.

## Authority Objection — Handle With Care
'Let me talk to my partner' is the most commonly used smokescreen in the book. Before routing this as a true authority objection, isolate it thoroughly.

## ⚠️ AUTHORITY SMOKESCREEN WARNING
Ask yourself: Is this a real decision-maker constraint — or is this an escape hatch? A prospect who uses 'my partner' to get off the phone is showing you a fear or uncertainty objection, not a true authority issue. Isolate before routing.

## How to Handle Logistical Objections
Time: Acknowledge it's real. Set a concrete follow-up date. Do not push — stay warm and present until timing aligns.

Money: Explore whether funds can be accessed differently (bridge, payment structure, etc.). If not, set a specific date when they can — and hold the slot.

Authority: Get the third party on the next call. Do not allow a relay — a message passed through the prospect to a decision-maker always gets distorted.

## Isolation — The Step Before Any Reframe
Before attempting any reframe or response, you must isolate the objection. This is the single most important skill in objection handling. Responding to the wrong category is worse than not responding at all.

## The Isolation Process
## Step
## What to Do
### 1. Acknowledge
Let them feel heard. Don't rush to fix or rebut. Silence and acknowledgment open the door.

### 2. Clarify
Ask a clarifying question that reveals whether this is emotional or logical. Example: 'Help me understand — what specifically concerns you about [X]?'

### 3. Confirm the Type
Is their answer emotional and irrational? Fear-based. Is it a gap in belief? Uncertainty. Is it a real constraint? Logistical.

### 4. Confirm It's the Only One
Before you handle it: 'Is that the only thing standing in the way, or is there something else?' Surface all objections before reframing any.

### 5. Respond with the Right Reframe
Now — and only now — apply the appropriate handling method for the category.

## 📌 RULE
Confirming it's the only objection before reframing is non-negotiable. Handle one objection. They say 'Actually, there's also...' You just wasted your best material. Get everything on the table first.
