Because they're relatable. They shift your reader's perspective. They implant beliefs that take them from "Meh" about your offer to "I need this"...

All through this unique but simple style of email storytelling I created.

### Let's get right into it, shall we?
## The Money Tales Framework
### Every profitable story you tell needs three core elements:
### 1. Purpose (Why Are You Sending this Email?)
This is the foundation of your story. Before you write anything, you need to be crystal clear on what this story is meant to accomplish. Without purpose, you're just telling random anecdotes.

### Examples of a purpose:
### To explain who, why and what you are
## To gain authority or credibility
## To incrementally establish a backstory
### To empathize & create a bond
## To appear human and thus believable
## To present an inconvenient truth
## To present a philosophy
### To teach or glorify a value we need them to believe in to buy
## To build up courage
### 2. Principle (The Moral of the Story)
This is where you separate yourself from everyone else. Most people just share what happened—you extract the deeper lesson.

### A principle…
Gives your audience new perspectives or a new way to think about things (including your offer)

## Creates transferable knowledge they can apply anywhere
Is WAY more valuable than "how to" according to the latest science — (Schema Theory, Cognitive Flexibility Theory, Transfer of Learning - see below)

### 3. Story (How You Hook Them In)
Your story is the vehicle that delivers your purpose and principle in an entertaining, memorable way.

Your story can and SHOULD be normal and mundane. Throwing in some wild or out of the ordinary stories here and there are ok, but most should be normal stuff, because it's RELATABLE!

Either way, it needs to be entertaining. You can make anything entertaining. It's not what story you tell, it's HOW you tell it.

Find the story that fits the purpose and principle. If it doesn't fit, discard.

## The 5-Step Money Tales Script
Clear on the 3 core elements of a Money Tale? Good. Now, let's look at how it's all arranged—step-by-step:

Step 1: Hook Create curiosity with an intriguing headline (or subject line) that makes people stop scrolling.

Step 2: What Happened (Your Story) Tell your story in an engaging way. Remember: mundane moments work best because they're relatable.

Step 3: Principle (The Moral) Extract the deeper lesson or new way of thinking from your story.

Step 4: Transition Bridge from your principle to your offer smoothly and naturally.

Step 5: Call to Action Direct your audience to take the next step.

## Money Tale Example
### Hook: My client makes millions but does this one thing like it's 1985
Story: Multi-millionaire client still writes physical checks for everything and mails them. Takes him hours each month. His bank offers automatic payments but he "likes to stay in control."

Principle: People do things the hard way because they're used to it. They aren't comfortable with the unfamiliar, even if it's more effective.

### 3 Scientific Reasons that PROVE Why You Should Never Teach "How To"
## Schema Theory
When you teach principles instead of tactics, you help people create "master keys" for their mental filing cabinets. Tactics are like adding a single paper to one folder—specific but limited. Principles are like master keys that unlock multiple folders, making it easier to retrieve information, connect ideas, and apply knowledge to new situations.

## Cognitive Flexibility Theory
Teaching principles is like giving someone a "super app" for their brain. Tactics are single-use apps that only work for one job. Principles are super apps that help you understand how different problems work, so you can figure out solutions even for situations you've never encountered before.

## Transfer of Learning
Principles teach people "how to learn" and "how to think." If life were a video game, tactics would be learning special moves for one character in one level. Principles teach you how to understand any video game quickly, no matter what world you're in—knowledge that transfers to any situation you face.

## How to Write Your Own Money Tale Story Today
→ Look at your day and identify one ordinary moment or experience.

### → Ask yourself: "What lesson or principle can I extract from this?"
→ Create a curious hook that makes people want to know more.

→ Tell your story in a few sentences. Keep it engaging yet logical so they can follow.

→ Connect your principle to what your audience needs to believe.

→ Transition smoothly to your offer or call to action.
