# Waiz Media Money Model And Offer Architecture

## Purpose

This document defines how Waiz Media makes money: the offer structure, routing logic, buyer profiles, downsell role, and business rules that govern how prospects move through the sales system.

## Scope

This is the canonical reference for company-level offer architecture and sales routing logic.

Use this document when creating sales scripts, objection handling, offer framing, qualification criteria, follow-up logic, AI prompts, or any workflow involving pricing escalation and offer presentation.

This document does not contain live pricing. Pricing changes frequently and must be referenced from the separate pricing sheet.

## Owner

Operations owns the structure of this document. Gabe owns final decisions on pricing, deal structure, and qualification for the core offer.

## When To Use

Use this document when:

- Building or revising sales scripts.
- Writing prompts that touch sales, pricing, offer presentation, or qualification.
- Training setters, closers, or AI agents on offer routing.
- Auditing whether prospects are being routed correctly.
- Designing nurture flows for prospects who do not buy DFY immediately.

## Offers

Waiz Media operates a two-offer money model. Every prospect is routed toward one of two offers based on qualification.

The offers are not interchangeable. They serve different buyers at different stages of readiness and commitment.

## Core Offer: Done-For-You Client Acquisition System

The core offer is a fully managed acquisition system built exclusively for reverse mortgage loan officers.

Waiz Media handles the front end of the client's business so the loan officer can focus on closing loans.

### Included Components

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| Inbound Lead Generation | Meta paid ad campaigns using educational video content, not generic stock-image ads. |
| Lead Qualification And Nurture | Team-led pre-qualification plus automated email and SMS workflows. |
| Reverse Sales Assistant | A dedicated team calls new leads quickly, pre-qualifies them, and books them to the loan officer's calendar. |
| AI Booking System | Automated follow-up, lead nurture, and database re-engagement that runs without loan officer involvement. |
| Custom CRM | GoHighLevel CRM customized for the reverse mortgage niche and the specific client. |
| KPI Tracking And Reporting | Full-funnel visibility into conversions, performance, and reporting. |
| Dedicated Support Team | Ongoing technical support, strategic guidance, and operational assistance. |

## Downsell Offer: 5-Day Ad-Building Boot Camp

The Boot Camp is a low-cost, high-value offer for prospects who are not yet qualified for, or not ready to commit to, the core DFY service.

It should be positioned as the right fit for the prospect's current stage, not as a consolation prize.

### What The Boot Camp Does

| Function | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Immediate revenue recovery | Converts disqualified or not-yet-ready prospects into paying customers instead of dropping them entirely. |
| Pipeline building | Moves graduates into the Waiz Media community and nurtures them toward DFY over time. |
| Business asset creation | Builds a warm audience that has already experienced Waiz Media value, reducing future CAC for DFY. |

The Boot Camp is currently operational on Skool. It teaches reverse mortgage loan officers how to build and run their own Meta ad campaigns.

## Pricing Rule

Pricing for both offers lives on a separate pricing sheet and is not included in this document.

Pricing is founder-only. Setters should never quote, negotiate, or discuss pricing or deal structure. Pricing questions escalate to Gabe immediately.

## Buyer Profiles

| Category | Core DFY Offer | Boot Camp Downsell |
|---|---|---|
| Who they are | Established reverse mortgage loan officer with an active book of business. | Loan officer not yet ready for DFY: newer, budget-constrained, or not fully convinced. |
| Current state | Referral-dependent, inconsistent pipeline, and likely burned by previous marketing attempts. | Lacks resources, is skeptical of agencies, or is still building baseline marketing knowledge. |
| What they want | Consistent qualified conversations, predictable closed deals, and less dependence on referrals. | Foundational skills and confidence to run campaigns before committing to DFY. |
| Core fear | Being burned again by an agency that does not understand the product or niche. | Wasting money on a service they are not ready to use or trust. |
| What they are buying | Security and control through a predictable system. | Proof of concept and a lower-risk entry into Waiz Media's ecosystem. |

## Offer Routing Logic

Every prospect is routed at two major decision points: the intro call and the demo call.

The Boot Camp is not a fallback. It is a deliberate offer presented to every prospect who does not qualify for the core DFY service.

| Decision Point | If Qualified | If Not Qualified |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1: Intro Call, owned by Pedro | Book a demo call with Gabe. | Present the Boot Camp downsell. Do not drop the prospect without an offer. |
| Stage 2: Demo Call, owned by Gabe | Close on the DFY retainer. | Route to the Boot Camp downsell. Do not drop the prospect without an offer. |
| Post-Demo, unconverted | Run both tracks: automated indoctrination sequence and Pedro manual follow-up. | Continue until close, opt-out, or Boot Camp handoff. |

## Business Rule: No Prospect Is Dropped

At every exit point in the funnel, the Boot Camp offer must be presented.

This is a business rule, not a suggestion. Both the setter and closer are responsible for the handoff.

## Downsell As A Business Asset

The Boot Camp performs three roles inside the business model:

1. Revenue recovery: disqualified prospects can still become paying customers.
2. Pipeline building: Boot Camp graduates enter the community and are nurtured toward DFY.
3. CAC reduction: the community becomes a warm, pre-sold audience for future DFY enrollment.

The intended upsell path is:

Boot Camp -> community -> DFY when ready.

## Operating Rules

| Rule | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pricing is founder-only | Setters never quote, negotiate, or discuss pricing or deal structure. Escalate pricing questions to Gabe. |
| Always attempt the downsell | No disqualified prospect leaves without a Boot Camp offer first. |
| Closer re-qualifies independently | Gabe makes the final qualification decision at the demo, even if Pedro marked the prospect qualified. |
| Both post-demo tracks run in parallel | Automated indoctrination and Pedro manual follow-up happen at the same time. |
| DFY clients are exclusive | There are no shared lead pools. Every client's leads are exclusive to that client. |
| Waiz Media serves reverse mortgage LOs only | No forward mortgage, generalist loan officer, or other niche offers. |
| No revenue share or pay-later deals | Revenue share and work-now-pay-later arrangements are not presented or negotiated. |
| Market the problem, not the product name | Ads should lead with homeowner retirement problems or outcomes, not the phrase "reverse mortgage." |

## Long-Term Model Vision

The current model has two revenue streams: the DFY retainer as the primary offer and Boot Camp as the secondary offer.

The long-term model uses the community as a larger acquisition and nurture asset.

| Area | Current State | Long-Term Target |
|---|---|---|
| Primary lead source | Paid Meta ads driving inbound traffic to landing pages. | Community as the primary organic lead engine for DFY. |
| DFY CAC | Paid ad spend plus setter cost per closed deal. | Lower CAC as warm community members self-select into DFY. |
| Boot Camp role | Downsell that recovers revenue from disqualified prospects. | Primary front-end offer and community feeder at scale. |
| Upsell path | Pedro manually follows up with post-demo unconverted prospects. | Structured community-to-DFY nurture system. |

## Quality Bar

Sales, marketing, and AI outputs that reference the money model should:

- Route every prospect to either DFY or Boot Camp.
- Avoid quoting pricing unless the source is the current approved pricing sheet.
- Preserve Gabe's final authority over pricing and DFY qualification.
- Never position Boot Camp as a consolation prize.
- Reinforce Waiz Media's exclusive focus on reverse mortgage loan officers.
- Avoid presenting revenue share, pay-later, or shared-lead models as options.

## Metrics

Track these metrics to evaluate the money model:

- Intro call qualification rate.
- Demo booking rate.
- Demo close rate.
- Boot Camp offer rate after disqualification.
- Boot Camp conversion rate.
- Boot Camp-to-community activation rate.
- Community-to-DFY conversion rate.
- DFY CAC.
- DFY client LTV.
- Percentage of prospects dropped without an offer.
