# Nurture Framework

> **North star:** Sales success is follow-up — ads only start the conversation; nurturing is what turns interest into booked appointments and closed loans.

Flow-agnostic principles for **why** follow-up matters, **what beliefs to break**, **how to run cadence**, and **how to reactivate aged leads. Application-specific stacks (Waiz bot, Meta drip, GHL builds) link here — they do not live in this doc.

## Purpose

Give loan officers and their teams a shared mental model for lead nurturing that applies across channels and workflows. Client education modules and execution scripts link to this framework instead of duplicating principles.

## 1. Why nurturing matters for reverse mortgage

### The core truth

**Conversion is not an event — it is a process.** Between form fill and funded loan, the lead passes through interest → education → trust → appointment → decision. Every gap where follow-up stops is a leak — and in reverse mortgage, those leaks are expensive because **cycles are long and trust is low**.

Ads and funnels create **opportunity**. Nurture and sales follow-up **capture** it. When LOs say "the leads are bad," the diagnosis is often **under-nurture**, not under-advertising.

### Why RM is harder than most sales cycles

| Factor | Effect on follow-up |
|--------|---------------------|
| **Trust deficit** | Product name triggers fear before you speak; education must run before pressure |
| **Slow decision style** | Borrowers research for weeks/months; silence on Day 3 is normal |
| **D2C Meta leads** | High peak interest, low context — they may not remember your name tomorrow |
| **Family involvement** | Spouse or adult children often join the decision — extends timeline |
| **Debt frame default** | Features sound like traps until the retirement-tool frame is built |

Client teaching module: [RM Borrower ICP — Client Education](../course-material/rm-borrower-icp-education.md).

### Rules (Section 1)

| Rule | Why |
|------|-----|
| **Treat follow-up as sales** | The LO who wins is usually the one who stayed in touch — not the one with the best ad |
| **Never diagnose on touch 1–2** | Most revenue sits after the 5th contact in high-consideration sales |
| **Speed on peak interest** | First minutes after form fill are the highest-motivation window |
| **Education before close** | RM borrowers need frame shift (debt → retirement tool), not a hard pitch |
| **Volume is ethical** | If they opted in, they asked for help — giving up early does them a disservice |

### Data backing (why volume and speed matter)

| Insight | Takeaway for LOs | Source |
|---------|------------------|--------|
| Most conversions cluster around the **5th–12th** contact | Don't stop at 1–3 touches | Industry nurture benchmarks; Waiz course material |
| **~44%** of salespeople stop after the **first** attempt; average **~1.3** attempts before giving up | Your persistence is a competitive advantage | InsideSales.com / RAIN Group (via Hormozi $100M Lead Nurture) |
| Contact in **≤5 minutes** vs hours/days dramatically changes outcomes | Work fresh leads same day; use bot/assistant for instant first touch when available | InsideSales.com; Waiz speed-to-lead standard |
| Faster first contact → **fewer** total touches needed to convert | Speed reduces how hard you must grind later | Hormozi $100M Lead Nurture — Pillar II |
| **Conversion is a process**, not one call | Own the full arc form → book → show → close | [Doctrine Reverse Mortgage](../reverse-mortgage-dna/doctrine-reverse-mortgage.md) — full-funnel ownership |

Mark unverified client-facing stats `[TO FILL]` until you approve specific numbers for LO materials.

### RM-specific failure mode

The industry default: stock creative → cold form → **single-call close attempt** → "leads don't work."

Waiz doctrine: education-first marketing + **owned follow-up** fixes the leaky bucket. Nurture is not optional overhead — it is the mechanism that makes D2C RM economically viable.

## The four pillars (universal)

Every nurture system — automated, manual, or mixed — needs all four. Weakness in one shows up as "bad leads" or "ads don't work."

| Pillar | Question it answers | Rule |
|--------|---------------------|------|
| **I — Availability** | Can they take the next step immediately? | If they cannot schedule or act now, they cannot show |
| **II — Speed** | How fast do you show up after interest peaks? | First contact in **≤5 minutes** when possible; gold standard under 60 seconds |
| **III — Personalization** | Does follow-up feel relevant to *their* situation? | Reference what they asked for; outcome-first, not generic blast |
| **IV — Volume** | Do you quit before the lead is ready? | Plan for **5–12+** contacts in high-consideration sales; silence ≠ rejection on Day 3 |

**Waiz DFY clients:** automated Meta stack maps to these pillars in the client portal — not in this framework. See [course material appendix](../course-material/lead-nurture-playbook.md#appendix--waiz-dfy-clients-only) if you are a paying client.

## 2. Limiting beliefs to break

LO teams often quit follow-up because of **internal** beliefs — not because the lead is dead. This section breaks those beliefs. **Prospect beliefs on live calls:** universal [Conceptual Beliefs Framework](../../acquisition/sales/conceptual-beliefs-framework.md) · RM application [Conceptual Beliefs Playbook](../client-sales/playbook-rm-conceptual-beliefs.md). Borrower word tracks: [RM Borrower Objections](../reverse-mortgage-dna/rm-borrower-objections.md).

### Belief 1: "They ghosted — they're gone"

**Reframe:** Silence usually means **interrupted**, not rejected. RM borrowers decide slowly ([ICP education](../course-material/rm-borrower-icp-education.md)); D2C leads often don't recognize your number; phones and carriers block unknown callers routinely.

| What you assume | What may actually be happening |
|-----------------|--------------------------------|
| "Not interested" | Still researching; talking to family; life got in the way |
| "Bad lead" | Your number is blocked, silenced, or sent to voicemail-only |
| "I called enough" | You stopped at touch 1–2; most competitors do ([§1 stats](#data-backing-why-volume-and-speed-matter)) |

**Rules**

| Rule | Action |
|------|--------|
| **Ghost ≠ gone** | Keep nurture running per cadence (automated + manual) until explicit exit — not silence alone |
| **Multi-channel** | If calls fail, use SMS, email, and alternate lines — same person, different paths |
| **Multiple lines** | Rotate or use local/published LO lines when primary number is blocked or flagged spam |
| **Reach like you mean it** | If they opted in, contact with the same effort you'd use to reach family in an emergency — within compliance and frequency norms |

Do **not** interpret no-answer on Day 2 as disqualification. Log the touch, schedule the next attempt, switch channel if needed.

**Borrower-side ghosting:** Silence can also mean an **unchecked conceptual belief** — they weren't ready, not that follow-up failed. See [Belief gaps and ghosting](#belief-gaps-and-ghosting) below and [RM Conceptual Beliefs Playbook](../client-sales/playbook-rm-conceptual-beliefs.md).

### Belief 2: "There's nothing I can do to prevent ghosting"

**Reframe:** You cannot eliminate ghosting — but you can **stack the odds** before and between conversations.

| Prevention tactic | When | Why it works |
|-------------------|------|----------------|
| **Save your contact** | First live conversation | They recognize your name on the next ring; reduces block/ignore rate |
| **Install beliefs on every live call** | Discovery before pitch | Unchecked Pain, Trust, Cost, or Support → they disengage later even if you follow up perfectly |
| **BAMFAM** | End of every call that doesn't book | Book the *next* step before hanging up — see [§3 Cadence science](#3-cadence-science) and [BAMFAM Playbook — RM](../client-sales/playbook-bamfam-rm.md) *(Section 3)* |
| **No gap between slots** | After a booked call | Days or weeks between setter touch and LO close = motivation dies; pull forward or confirm same-week |
| **Speed of response** | After they reply mid-sequence | Slow reply trains them you're not serious — see Pillar II in [Four pillars](#the-four-pillars-universal) |

**BAMFAM preview (detail in §3):** *Book a meeting from a meeting* — never "I'll call you later" without a calendar hold. If they can't talk now, book the soonest realistic slot **while still on the phone**.

### Belief 3: "Objections mean no — I should stop"

**Reframe:** Borrowers arrive in the **debt frame** ([Doctrine RM Marketing](../reverse-mortgage-dna/doctrine-rm-marketing.md)). What sounds like rejection is often a **missing conceptual belief** — not a final answer. You cannot install what you don't diagnose.

| Step | What to do |
|------|------------|
| 1 | **Map the belief ladder** — [RM Conceptual Beliefs Playbook](../client-sales/playbook-rm-conceptual-beliefs.md) + universal [Conceptual Beliefs Framework](../../acquisition/sales/conceptual-beliefs-framework.md) |
| 2 | **Listen for the gap** behind the words ("bank takes the house" = Trust; "I'll wait until rates drop" = Cost unchecked) |
| 3 | **Stay in discovery** — Socratic questions until *they* articulate the empowering belief; don't pitch through a gap |
| 4 | **Navigate with objections doc** — [RM Borrower Objections](../reverse-mortgage-dna/rm-borrower-objections.md) when pushback still surfaces — diagnose which belief was missed |
| 5 | **Stay in follow-up** — one belief gap on touch 3 does not close a 90-day RM cycle; nurture educates until beliefs check |

### Belief gaps and ghosting

Not every ghost is operational (blocked number, bad timing). Many borrowers **leave nurture** because a belief was never installed:

| Unchecked belief | How it shows up in nurture |
|------------------|----------------------------|
| **Pain** | "We're fine for now" — low urgency; stops engaging after initial curiosity |
| **Doubt** | Shops forever; goes silent comparing HELOC, sale, or "figure it out alone" |
| **Cost** | "I'll wait" — deferral feels safer than acting; classic if-when without a calendar hold |
| **Support** | Ghosts until spouse or adult children are aligned — never booked joint call |
| **Trust** | One scary detail → disengages; won't return calls until fear is addressed |
| **Desire / Money** | Vague interest but no emotional win or value clarity — polite no or silence |

**Nurture implication:** Follow-up volume is necessary but not sufficient. Each touch should **move one belief** — education, personalization, joint-call booking — not repeat the same pitch. Log which beliefs are checked vs gap on every live conversation ([beliefs playbook worksheet](../client-sales/playbook-rm-conceptual-beliefs.md)).

**LO mistake to avoid:** Treating belief-gap silence as "bad lead" and quitting — when the fix is more discovery on the next call, not more pressure.

### Rules summary (Section 2)

| # | Rule |
|---|------|
| 1 | Silence is not disqualification — exhaust volume pillar before you judge lead quality |
| 2 | Blocked numbers are common — multi-line, multi-channel outreach |
| 3 | First call: ask them to save your contact name/number |
| 4 | Never end a live call without next step booked or BAMFAM scheduled |
| 5 | Diagnose belief gaps on every live call → then use objection playbook — not the reverse |

## 3. Cadence science

Cadence is **when** and **how often** you work leads — and how fast you book the next step. Three speed layers + dial discipline + BAMFAM. Universal rules here; step-by-step execution in linked SOPs.

> **Overlap guard:** Speed/volume *stats* live in [§1](#data-backing-why-volume-and-speed-matter) and [Four pillars](#the-four-pillars-universal). BAMFAM *beliefs* preview in [§2](#2-limiting-beliefs-to-break). This section owns **operating cadence**.

### The three speeds

| Speed | Definition | Rule |
|-------|------------|------|
| **Speed to first contact** | Time from opt-in to first call/text | **≤5 minutes** on fresh leads when humanly possible; automation can cover instant first touch when configured |
| **Speed to first appointment** | Gap between interest and booked LO conversation | Book inside **72 hours**; prefer same/next day — farther out = higher ghost risk |
| **Speed of response** | Reply time after they engage (text, email, question) | Same hour on hot replies; never next-business-day on someone mid-conversation |

Faster first contact → **fewer total touches** needed later ([§1](#data-backing-why-volume-and-speed-matter)).

**Automation note:** When speed-to-lead is automated, LO dial blocks should complement — not compete with — the first touch.

### How to dial (framework rules)

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Work a queue** | Fresh → replies → callbacks → unreached → aged — no cherry-picking |
| **Double-dial** | Second immediate attempt when first rings out; many devices block call one |
| **VM + text same session** | Voicemail then short outcome-first text — not call-only |
| **Front-load Week 1** | Heavy attempt pattern Days 1–3; taper after ([LO Lead Dialing SOP](sop-lo-lead-dialing-rm.md)) |
| **Notes every touch** | Disposition + next step before moving to next lead |
| **Call before cold text** | On leads you haven't spoken with |

Full dial blocks, cadence table, dispositions: **[LO Lead Dialing SOP — RM](sop-lo-lead-dialing-rm.md)**.

### BAMFAM (framework rules)

**BAMFAM** = book the next meeting **from** the meeting (or any live call). Core anti-ghost tactic.

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Never "call you later" without a calendar hold | Phone tag kills RM show rates |
| Offer two concrete slots | Not open-ended "when works for you?" |
| Pull forward when possible | Same-day / next-day beats next week |
| Confirm on the phone | Read back time, timezone, LO name |
| Save contact on first live call | Pairs with [§2](#2-limiting-beliefs-to-break) |

Word tracks, five outcomes, reminders: **[BAMFAM Playbook — RM](../client-sales/playbook-bamfam-rm.md)**.

### Rules summary (Section 3)

| # | Rule |
|---|------|
| 1 | Fresh leads same day — speed to first contact is non-negotiable |
| 2 | Book LO conversations inside 72 hours when qualified |
| 3 | Double-dial + VM + text per [dialing SOP](sop-lo-lead-dialing-rm.md) |
| 4 | BAMFAM every live call that doesn't transfer or close |
| 5 | Reply fast when they re-engage — don't train them you're slow |

## 4. Aged lead reactivation

### The core reframe: "New opportunity"

Aged leads are not failures — they are **deferred decisions**. The borrower who didn't book in Week 1 may still be researching, may have missed your calls, or may have had life interrupt the process. **Circumstances also change** — equity grows, bills rise, family alignment shifts, other options fall through.

Your outreach is not "bugging them again." It is a **new opportunity check-in**: *has your situation changed enough that a short conversation is worth it now?*

Distilled from industry nurture practice (Hormozi $100M Lead Nurture — Volume pillar): *"In the case of older leads, maybe they didn't need your stuff then, but they do now — and you gave them a nice reminder."*

### Why RM aged leads are worth the work

| Factor | Implication |
|--------|-------------|
| **Equity grows over time** | Numbers they dismissed months ago may look different today |
| **Fixed-income pressure** | Bills, medical, and repairs don't wait for your sequence to finish |
| **Prior opt-in = permission** | They asked for contact — ethical follow-up includes re-engagement |
| **Competitors quit early** | Most LOs stop after Week 1; persistence is advantage ([§1 stats](#data-backing-why-volume-and-speed-matter)) |
| **RM trust takes time** | Silence after education touches is normal — not disqualification |

### Segment before you dial

| Segment | Definition | Reactivation angle |
|---------|------------|-------------------|
| **Never reached** | Opted in; no live conversation | Delayed fresh lead — urgency without guilt |
| **Reached, no book** | Talked; no appointment | Reference prior motivator; BAMFAM |
| **Booked, no show** | Had slot; didn't appear | Low-friction rebook — no shame |
| **Engaged, no close** | LO conversation; went cold | Timing / numbers / life-change hook |
| **Nurture-only** | Automation only; no manual touch | Human call breaks pattern |

Full word tracks: **[Aged Lead Reactivation Script — RM](script-aged-lead-reactivation-rm.md)**.

### Framework rules (Section 4)

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Lead with value or change** | News or timing check — not "sorry to bother you again" |
| **One clear ask** | Book a short call — same BAMFAM discipline as [§3](#3-cadence-science) |
| **Personalize from CRM** | Pull prior motivator, objection, spouse mention before dialing |
| **Lighter cadence than Week 1** | Reactivation burst, then space — don't spam aged lists |
| **Honor exits** | STOP, wrong number, hard disqualify → remove immediately |
| **Human + automated** | Long-term nurture keeps running; manual reactivation re-opens warm paths |

### LO beliefs to break (Section 4)

| If you think… | Replace with |
|---------------|--------------|
| "They're dead leads" | **Deferred** — circumstances and timing change |
| "I'd be annoying" | They opted in; a value check-in is service when done with respect |
| "I already tried" | Most RM revenue sits after touch 5+ — reactivation is part of volume |

### Rules summary (Section 4)

| # | Rule |
|---|------|
| 1 | Frame reactivation as **new opportunity**, not apology |
| 2 | Segment the list — angle changes per prior touch history |
| 3 | BAMFAM when they engage — book inside 72 hours |
| 4 | After structured attempts with no signal, return to long-term nurture |
| 5 | Compliance and unsubscribe rules are non-negotiable |
