## Operating Content

Drip Sequence: Reverse Mortgage Lead Nurture

Objective: To nurture qualified leads for a reverse mortgage by educating them, building trust, and overcoming common objections. The campaign is designed to be empathetic, compliant, and aligned with the brand's mission of providing clarity and dignity to retired homeowners.

DAY 1: Immediate Welcome & Value Delivery

Goal: Establish a helpful, human connection and set expectations.

### Text:

Hey {Name}, this is {Your Name}. I saw you were looking for information on securing your retirement finances and wanted to reach out personally. Just sent you something helpful — check your email! (Spam folder too, just in case.) 📧

### Email:

Subject: A different way to think about your retirement funding

Hi {Name},

Thank you for your interest. You're likely here because you're exploring ways to make your retirement more financially comfortable, and you suspect your home's equity might be the key.

Many retired homeowners feel the squeeze of a fixed income against rising costs. They've built incredible value in their homes, but it's locked away, leaving them feeling "house-rich and cash-poor."

Good news: there is a respected, FHA-insured program that allows homeowners 62 and older to access that equity, giving them the financial breathing room they need without having to sell their home.

Over the next few days, I'm going to share:

How this home-equity program actually works (in plain English).

Real stories from homeowners who used it to find peace of mind.

The built-in safeguards that protect you, your family, and your home.

My goal is simply to provide the facts so you can make a smart, unpressured decision.

If you have any questions at all, please reply to this email or text me at {Your Number}.

{Your Name} {Title} | {Company} {Phone} | {Email}

P.S. — The most common fear is, "Will I lose my house?" Let's clear that up tomorrow. The answer is a reassuring one.

DAY 2: Address the Core Objection: Ownership

Goal: Neutralize the biggest fear (losing the home) with clear, factual information.

### Text:

Hi {Name}, it's {Your Name}. Quick note on a question I hear all the time: you do NOT give your house to the bank. You keep the title, stay the owner, and your family still inherits the remaining equity. Hope that helps clarify things!

### Email:

Subject: Let's talk about who owns the home (hint: you do)

Hi {Name},

Let's address the biggest myth about this program head-on.

You've probably heard the horror stories or been told, "the bank takes your house."

Here is the truth for today's federally-insured home equity program (known as a HECM or reverse mortgage):

You keep the title. You remain the homeowner. You have the same rights and responsibilities as with a traditional mortgage.

Your heirs inherit the home and any remaining equity. They can choose to sell the home, pay the loan balance, and keep the difference, or they can refinance the loan and keep the home.

It is a non-recourse loan. This is a critical protection. It means you or your heirs will never owe more than the home is worth at the time the loan is repaid.

Think of it as a loan against your equity that you don't have to pay back with monthly payments. The balance is typically paid when you sell the home or pass away. Your family keeps whatever equity is left.

For example:

Home Value at time of sale: $450,000

Loan Balance: $250,000

Equity to Heirs: $200,000

Your legacy is protected. The goal of this program isn't to take something from you; it's to give you access to the wealth you've already built.

Does this make sense? I'm here to answer any questions.

{Your Name}

P.S. — Tomorrow, we'll explore what's possible when you free up your cash flow. It's about more than just paying bills.

DAY 3: Paint the Picture of Possibility

Goal: Shift from fear to aspiration. Help them visualize the "After" state.

### Text:

Hey {Name}, it's {Your Name}. Imagine waking up next month with no mortgage payment. What would you do with that extra cash flow? That's exactly what we're talking about — something worth thinking about!

### Email:

Subject: What would you do with an extra $2,000/month?

Hi {Name},

Let's set aside the technical details for a moment and talk about what this really means for your life.

If your required mortgage payment was gone tomorrow, what would that allow you to do? How would it make you feel?

Here's what other retired homeowners are doing with their newfound cash flow:

Finding Relief: Finally stopping the monthly drain on their savings and investments.

Making Upgrades: Taking care of that new roof, a more accessible bathroom, or a kitchen remodel.

Ensuring Health: Covering healthcare costs or in-home care to maintain their independence.

Creating Joy: Traveling to see family or simply enjoying retirement without guilt.

Gaining Control: Paying off high-interest credit cards or other nagging debts.

One couple I worked with eliminated their payment and set up a line of credit. They told me they sleep better at night just knowing they have a safety net for whatever life throws their way.

What would that kind of financial breathing room mean for you?

I'd genuinely love to hear what you're hoping to achieve.

{Your Name}

P.S. — Remember, you can receive the funds as a lump sum, monthly payments, a line of credit, or a combination. It's designed to be flexible for your needs.

DAY 4: Build Trust with a Relatable Story

Goal: Use social proof to show this is a solution for people just like them.

### Text:

Hey {Name}, it's {Your Name}. Just thought of a client story I wanted to share with you — I think it will resonate. Just sent it to your email. 👀

### Email:

Subject: "I wish we'd done this sooner"

Hi {Name},

I want to share a story about a couple I helped last year, Tom and Linda.

Their situation was common:

They had a $1,900/month mortgage payment.

They were living on a fixed income of about $3,500/month.

They were watching their savings dwindle to cover basic bills.

They felt stressed, trapped, and ashamed to tell their kids they were struggling.

They'd heard about this program but were afraid. They thought it was only for "desperate people."

After another year of anxiety, they decided to at least get the facts. Here's what happened:

We eliminated their $1,900 monthly mortgage payment, freeing up over $22,000 a year.

They established a standby line of credit for emergencies.

They finally felt in control of their finances again.

Six months later, Linda told me: "We finally feel like we can breathe. Our only regret is that we spent years struggling when we didn't have to."

They still own their home. Their children will still inherit a significant amount of equity. But now, Tom and Linda are actually living their retirement, not just surviving it.

If their story sounds familiar, let's talk. I can walk you through the numbers for your specific situation — no pressure, no obligation. Just clarity.

{Your Name}

DAY 5: Address the "Cost" Objection with Honesty

Goal: Transparently explain the costs and reframe them as an investment in cash flow.

### Text:

Hey {Name}, it's {Your Name}. People often ask me, "Aren't the fees high?" It's a fair question. The costs are typically rolled into the loan, and most people recoup them in savings within the first year. I sent a full breakdown to your email.

### Email:

Subject: Let's talk honestly about the costs

Hi {Name},

Whenever you're considering a financial tool, you should ask about the costs. Let's break them down with full transparency.

Yes, there are upfront costs, which typically include an origination fee, appraisal, title search, and FHA mortgage insurance. These are all regulated and will be clearly itemized for you.

But here's the crucial part that's often missed:

These costs are almost always rolled into the loan itself. You generally don't pay for them out of your pocket.

You are eliminating a required monthly payment. If you're paying $2,000/month now, that's $24,000 you'll be saving in the first year alone.

Let's look at the breakeven math:

Upfront Costs (Financed): $16,000

Monthly Payment Eliminated: $2,000

Breakeven Point: 8 months

After just 8 months, you'd be ahead by $24,000 per year in saved payments for as long as you live in the home.

The real question isn't, "Can I afford the fees?" It's, "Can I afford to keep sending out thousands of dollars in payments every year for the rest of my retirement?"

If you'd like to see a personalized, no-cost scenario for your situation, just reply and let me know. I'm happy to put one together for you.

{Your Name}

P.S. — For homeowners who plan to stay in their home for at least a few years, the math is almost always overwhelmingly positive.

DAY 6: Address the Legacy & Heirs Concern

Goal: Reassure them that their legacy is more than just money and that their family is protected.

### Text:

Hey {Name}, it's {Your Name}. Worried about leaving less to your kids? I completely understand. But what I hear from the children of my clients might surprise you — check your email.

### Email:

Subject: What your children actually want for you

Hi {Name},

One of the most heartfelt concerns I hear is, "I want to leave a strong legacy for my children."

That is an honorable goal. Your home is a symbol of your life's work, and you want to pass that on.

But after hundreds of conversations with families, I've learned something profound about what that legacy truly means.

Your children don't want you to struggle. They don't want you to spend your retirement worrying about bills, skipping medical appointments, or feeling stressed, just to maximize their inheritance.

What they want is for you to be happy, safe, and independent.

One daughter recently told me, "I'd rather my mom use the money now to enjoy her life than for me to get a bigger check later. Her peace of mind is the most important thing."

Your legacy isn't just a number in a bank account. It's the memory of a parent who lived their final years with dignity and joy.

And remember, your heirs still receive the remaining equity. If your home is worth $500,000 and the loan balance is $200,000, they receive $300,000. That is a significant inheritance by any measure.

Does thinking about it this way help?

{Your Name}

P.S. — You can always choose to make voluntary payments on the loan if you want to preserve more equity. The program is flexible to meet your goals.

DAY 7: Create Urgency Based on the Cost of Inaction

Goal: Motivate a decision by highlighting the cost of delay, not through high-pressure tactics.

### Text:

Hey {Name}, it's {Your Name}. A thought: every month that goes by is another mortgage payment out the door. That's money you could be keeping. If you've been on the fence, exploring your options now is a smart step — no commitment required.

### Email:

Subject: The cost of waiting

Hi {Name},

I'm not going to create false urgency. This decision is too important for that.

But I do want to offer a clear perspective on the cost of doing nothing.

Every single month that you delay exploring your options is another month you write a check to your mortgage company. If your payment is $2,100, waiting another year will cost you $25,200 in payments you didn't have to make.

That's $25,200 that could have stayed in your pocket, been added to your savings, or been used for something that brings you joy.

I'm not asking you to decide today. I'm suggesting that now is the perfect time to get the facts.

Let's schedule a brief, 15-minute call. I'll run your numbers, answer your questions, and provide a clear, no-obligation summary of your specific options. Then, you can decide if it makes sense from a place of clarity.

Reply with a day and time that works for you.

{Your Name}

DAY 8: The Final Question: What's the Alternative?

Goal: Compel them to confront the reality of their current path vs. the new possibility.

### Text:

Hey {Name}, it's {Your Name}. A simple question: if nothing changes, where will you be a year from now? Still making those payments? Still stressed? Let's talk about a different path.

### Email:

Subject: A question for you

Hi {Name},

If you decide not to move forward, what does your life look like a year from now?

Will you still be:

Sending a large portion of your fixed income to a mortgage company?

Feeling anxious about unexpected home repairs or medical bills?

Putting off the travel and experiences you worked your whole life for?

Doing nothing is a choice, and it has a cost.

Choosing to get the facts is also a choice. It's a choice that opens up a different path—one where you have more cash flow, more control, and more peace of mind.

I've seen too many people wait until a crisis forces their hand. They have to act, instead of getting to choose.

Don't let that be you. Let's talk this week. I'll show you what's possible.

{Your Name}

I just want to reiterate that my role here is to educate, not to sell. Whether you move forward or not, my goal is for you to feel confident in your decision.

If you have any lingering questions — no matter how small — please ask. A 15-minute chat could provide the clarity you're looking for.

{Your Name}

P.S. — If you've decided this isn't for you, that's okay too. Just let me know and I'll stop reaching out. I wish you all the best either way.

Don't let confusion keep you stuck. If you have even one last question, I promise to give you a straight answer.

I wish you and your family all the best.
