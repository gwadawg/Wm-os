### Active model (do not edit here — edit the canonical bridge)

**Personas:** Widowed · Married Couple · Pre-Retiree  
**Archetypes:** Security-Seeker · Financially Squeezed · Strategic Retiree  
**Family / heirs / burden / living legacy:** triggers + myth content, not an archetype

### Why Legacy Planner was demoted

Purchase-psychology audit: heir concern is nearly universal (a gate that must
clear for any purchase), not a rare primary hiring job. When family *is* the
motive, the purchase is still cash-out with a family trigger. Strategic Retiree
and pressure buyers already need heir copy — a fourth peer archetype blurred
into Strategic and over-segmented the market.

## Archived: Legacy Planner as archetype (full doctrine text)

Core driver: Generosity and family

Who they are: Financially comfortable but motivated by what happens to their
wealth — or what they can do for family right now. Looks at home equity and
sees an opportunity to create a living legacy: help adult children, secure a
spouse's future, fund a grandchild's education, or make a meaningful gift while
still alive to see it received.

What moves them: Family and legacy language. The inheritance question must be
answered directly and confidently — not buried. Involving adult children in the
process, rather than treating them as a barrier, increases close rate
significantly.

Product frame: Legacy instrument — deploy equity in service of the people they
care about.

**Use:** living legacy · secure spouse's future · gift while you can see them
enjoy it · heirs protected · non-recourse  

**Never:** imply inheritance at risk without context · dismiss heir concerns ·
purely personal benefit only · vague answers to inheritance · pitchy tone

**How to use this content now:** brief `trigger: heirs | burden | living-legacy`
on Widowed or Married Couple, with archetype Security-Seeker / Financially
Squeezed / Strategic Retiree as appropriate. Keep heir myth-bust in MOF.
