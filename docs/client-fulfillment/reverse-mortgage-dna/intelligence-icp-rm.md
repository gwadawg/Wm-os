1. Who Is This Person

Your ideal customer is not someone shopping for a financial product. They are a house-rich, cash-poor homeowner aged 62+ living in a state of quiet financial desperation. They feel trapped — owning a home worth hundreds of thousands of dollars while watching their savings disappear month by month.

They are not buying a reverse mortgage. They are buying relief, dignity, and the freedom to stay in the home they love.

2. Before & After States

All marketing must paint the contrast between where they are now (the pain) and where they want to be (the dream). This contrast is the engine behind every high-converting ad, email, and conversation.

The 'Before' State — Their Current Reality

Financial Situation

Living on Social Security + a fixed pension

Watching savings drain every single month

Credit cards slowly creeping up

Choosing between groceries, utilities, or medications

Property taxes, insurance, HOA fees increasing every year

No emergency fund remaining

One big expense (roof, medical, car) away from disaster

Home value rising — but zero access to it

Cannot qualify for traditional refinancing

Lifestyle & Practical Stress

Living extremely frugal despite owning a valuable asset

Staying home more because going out costs money

Delaying home repairs — roof, AC, plumbing deteriorating

Downsizing feels emotionally impossible (memories, family proximity)

Feels "stuck" — not by choice, but by circumstance

The 'After' State — What They're Secretly Hoping For

Financial Freedom

Monthly cash flow without stress

Bills paid without juggling

An emergency fund that's actually there when needed

Access to home equity without having to sell or move

Confidence they won't outlive their money

Lifestyle & Emotional Dreams

Fixing the house — roof, HVAC, accessibility features

Traveling to see grandkids without guilt

Enjoying retirement instead of just surviving it

Living with dignity and independence

Not asking their children for help

The Golden Rule of Reverse Mortgage Marketing

They are not buying a loan or a financial product. They are buying: Peace of Mind · Control · Stability · Dignity · Independence · Security · Relief. Every piece of marketing must sell the outcome, not the mechanism.

7. Top 10 Objections & How to Address Them

Preemptively handle these in ad copy, landing pages, and nurture sequences. Understanding the fear behind the objection is as important as the response.

Objection

The Fear Behind It

How to Address It in Marketing

"The bank will take my house."

Loss of home and security

You remain the owner. As long as you live there and meet basic responsibilities, the home stays yours.

"My kids will inherit debt."

Being a burden; guilt

Your children are never personally responsible. The loan is repaid from the home's value when sold — not from their pockets.

"This will affect my Social Security or Medicare."

Loss of essential benefits

The money received is generally not considered income and does not impact Social Security or Medicare.

"I've heard horror stories."

Distrust; fear of scams

Most horror stories come from older, unregulated programs. Today's version is federally insured and heavily regulated.

"This sounds too good to be true."

Skepticism; fear of hidden costs

This isn't magic — it's accessing equity you've already built. We show you exactly how it works, including all costs.

"I don't want more debt."

Fear of financial burden

No required monthly payments. Instead of money going out every month, money comes in.

"What if I live a long time?"

Fear of running out of equity

You can never owe more than your home is worth, and you can never be forced to leave as long as you meet basic requirements.

"I want to leave my home to my kids."

Legacy concerns

Your heirs can keep the home by paying off the loan balance, or sell it. Any remaining equity goes to them.

"The fees are too high."

Fear of being taken advantage of

All fees are regulated and disclosed upfront. We walk you through every cost — no surprises.

"I need to think about it."

Decision paralysis; overwhelm

You should think about it. Our consultation is purely educational — so when you do think, you're thinking with clarity, not confusion.

The Master Prompt Template

### Role & Goal

You are a world-class direct response copywriter specializing in marketing to seniors (62+). Your goal is to generate ad copy that builds trust and encourages a qualified homeowner to request more information about a home equity program. Your tone is empathetic, educational, and respectful. You never use high-pressure tactics.

### Product Framing (Important)

A federally-insured Home Equity Conversion Mortgage (HECM). In consumer-facing copy, do NOT use the term 'reverse mortgage' as the headline. Instead use: 'home equity program,' 'cash out,' 'no monthly payment required,' or outcome-based language. This bypasses conditioned rejection responses.

9. Ethical Urgency & Scarcity Language

This audience responds to calm, protective urgency — not pressure tactics. All urgency should feel like a caring advisor helping them plan ahead, not a salesperson pushing them to act now.

What to Use

"To keep these conversations personal and unrushed, I only offer a limited number of homeowner consultations each week."

"Most homeowners start these conversations before finances become urgent — so they have more options."

"Home values and interest rates can change. Reviewing your options sooner gives you more flexibility."

"This is about planning — not reacting."

"Educational consultations are limited — availability fills quickly."

What to NEVER Use

❌ Avoid

Why It Backfires

"Last chance"

Creates panic; damages trust with this audience immediately

"Act now or else"

Triggers skepticism; sounds like a scam

"Limited time offer"

Implies a promotion — not educational; lowers credibility

"Don't miss out"

Pressure-based; this audience avoids pressure

"Free money"

Sounds fraudulent; immediately triggers rejection
