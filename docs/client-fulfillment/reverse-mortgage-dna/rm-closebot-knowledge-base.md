## A — Product overview

### What is a reverse mortgage (HECM)?

A **reverse mortgage** is a way to **refinance / access equity** in a home you already own — built for **retired homeowners**, with **no required monthly mortgage payment** on the new loan. Interest and fees are added to the loan balance over time. You keep title and remain the homeowner. The loan is generally repaid when you sell, move out, or the last borrower passes away.

If the homeowner still has a forward mortgage, it is typically **paid off at closing** with proceeds from the new loan — same refi mechanics, different loan structure.

### One-line summary

> Access equity in your home without a required monthly mortgage payment — and you stay the owner.

### How money can be taken

Lump sum, monthly payments (tenure or term), line of credit, or a combination. The unused HECM line of credit can grow over time on the unused portion.

### What homeowners use funds for

| Goal | How the program helps |
|------|----------------------|
| Eliminate monthly mortgage payment | Pay off existing mortgage at closing |
| Supplement retirement income | Monthly deposits if desired |
| Home repairs, medical, in-home care | Lump sum or line of credit |
| Debt consolidation | Lower monthly outflows |
| Emergency / strategic buffer | Standby line of credit |

### Quick reality checks

- You do not give the home to the bank — you keep ownership.
- The loan balance grows over time; equity decreases unless home appreciation outpaces it.
- Not ideal if planning to move soon or cannot keep up with taxes, insurance, and maintenance.

## B — Qualification basics

### How qualification works (high level — no numbers)

1. **Age** — typically 62+ (at least one borrower on title).
2. **Primary residence** — must live in the home.
3. **Equity** — enough equity to support the loan (existing mortgage can be paid off at closing).
4. **Property charges** — ability and willingness to keep up with taxes, insurance, and maintenance.
5. **Property type & condition** — must meet program standards.

**Key point:** No traditional FICO minimum for HECM. Credit is assessed in context of property-charge obligations — not income docs like a forward refi.

### Ongoing obligations after closing

- Live in the home as primary residence.
- Pay property taxes, homeowners insurance, and HOA dues.
- Maintain the property to program standards.
- Complete annual occupancy certification.
- Required **independent counseling** before closing (HECM).

## D — Key protections

| Protection | What to know |
|------------|--------------|
| **Ownership** | Borrower keeps title. The bank does not take the home while occupancy and property obligations are met. |
| **Non-recourse** | Borrower and heirs never owe more than the home's value when the loan is due. |
| **Heirs** | Heirs get remaining equity after the loan is paid. They can keep the home by paying off the balance or sell. |
| **Spousal protections** | Eligible non-borrowing spouses have protections under HECM rules. |
| **FHA-insured HECM** | Federally backed with required independent counseling before closing. |
| **Flexibility** | Can sell anytime; proceeds pay the balance and homeowner keeps remaining equity. No prepayment penalty for HECM. |

## E — Stigma & common myths

### Why people are skeptical

- The name **"reverse mortgage"** carries decades of negative association from old programs and media coverage.
- Most **horror stories** come from older, unregulated programs — not today's federally insured HECM.
- Many people reject the **name** before engaging with how the modern program works.
- The industry has a **trust deficit** — education must come before product name.

### Myth quick-reference

| Myth | Truth |
|------|-------|
| The bank takes my house | Borrower keeps title. Home stays theirs while they live there and meet taxes, insurance, and maintenance. |
| My kids will get nothing / inherit debt | Heirs are never personally liable. Non-recourse limits what is owed; remaining equity goes to heirs. |
| If home values drop, family owes a deficit | Non-recourse — neither borrower nor heirs owe more than home value when the loan is due. |
| No payments = forced payments later | No **required** monthly mortgage payment. Interest accrues to balance; optional payments anytime. |
| I can outlive the loan | Loan does not come due just because borrower lives longer — as long as they live there and meet obligations. |
| I must be mortgage-free | Can have existing mortgage; typically paid off at closing with proceeds. |
| Bad credit disqualifies me | No traditional FICO floor; lenders assess property-charge ability. |
| Reverse mortgages are scams | HECMs are FHA-insured, require independent counseling, and carry firm consumer protections. |
| Only for people in financial trouble | Many use strategically — remove a payment, build standby line of credit, fund care or repairs. |
| Once I take it, I cannot sell or move | Can sell anytime; proceeds pay balance and homeowner keeps remaining equity. |
| Taking one means I failed financially | Retirement cash-flow tool for house-rich homeowners optimizing liquidity. |
| Proceeds are taxable income | Loan advances, generally not taxable income — tax pro should confirm. |
| This will reduce Social Security or Medicare | SS and Medicare generally not impacted. Means-tested programs (Medicaid, SSI) may be affected if cash retained raises assets above thresholds. |

## F — ICP & borrower psychology

### Who this helps

**Retired homeowners** who are **house-rich and cash-flow-tight** — significant equity, fixed income (Social Security + pension), savings depleted or under pressure.

| Dimension | Profile |
|-----------|---------|
| Home | Paid-off or low balance; values rising but no access to equity |
| Income | Fixed — cannot qualify for traditional refinancing |
| Trust | Extremely low toward financial professionals; must be earned through education |
| Decision style | Slow, cautious; researches weeks or months; family input matters |
| Barriers | Fear of scams, family skepticism, confusion about how it works |

### Before state (their pain)

- Living on fixed income while bills and property costs rise.
- Savings draining month by month; credit cards creeping up.
- Choosing between groceries, utilities, or medications.
- Home repairs delayed; feeling stuck in a valuable asset they cannot use.
- Fear of becoming a burden to adult children.

### After state (what they want)

- Monthly cash flow without stress; bills paid without juggling.
- Emergency fund that is actually there.
- Fix the house; travel to see grandkids; enjoy retirement instead of surviving it.
- Live with dignity and independence — not asking children for help.

### Personas

| Persona | Core fear | What resonates |
|---------|-----------|----------------|
| **Widowed homeowner** | Alone, scared, doesn't want to leave memories | Security, staying in the home, independence |
| **Married couple** | Worked hard, still stressed every month | Enjoy retirement together; legacy matters |
| **Veteran** | Proud, skeptical of "handouts" | Independence — strategic tool, not charity |

### Emotional pain pairs (for empathy — not to recite)

| Situation | Identity-level impact |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Can't afford groceries or medication | Loss of dignity; fear-based decisions |
| Delaying home repairs | Living in worry; feeling unsafe at home |
| Fear of outliving savings | Long-term anxiety; time feels like the enemy |
| Shame about struggling | Self-blame; resistance to asking for help |
| Confusion about options | Decision paralysis; distrust of professionals |

## H — HEI / equity-sharing

### What HEI is (product knowledge)

**Home equity investment (HEI)** / **shared equity** / **equity-sharing agreement:** A company provides cash upfront in exchange for a share of the home's **future value**. It is an **investment**, not an FHA-insured loan. Repayment typically happens when the home is sold or at end of term.

### How HECM differs (product knowledge)

| | HEI / equity-sharing | HECM reverse mortgage |
|--|----------------------|----------------------|
| Structure | Investment in future home value | Federally insured loan against equity |
| Ownership | Homeowner keeps title (terms vary by provider) | Borrower keeps title |
| Monthly payments | Typically none | No required monthly mortgage payment |
| Consumer protections | Varies by provider and contract | FHA-insured; required independent counseling; non-recourse |
| Repayment | Share of appreciation / buyout at sale or term | Loan repaid on sell, move out, or last borrower passes |
| Legacy impact | Equity share reduces what stays in the estate | Heirs get remaining equity after loan paid; non-recourse cap |

### Why people confuse them

Both are marketed around **accessing equity** and **no monthly payments**. The differences that matter long-term: how much equity is retained, when and how repayment happens, consumer protections, and impact on heirs.

### How to respond (knowledge only — prompt handles tone and booking)

- **Stay neutral.** Never disparage HEI providers by name or say one option is "better."
- **Acknowledge** they are doing the right thing by researching.
- **Explain** the structures are different — investment vs federally insured loan program.
- **Note** the right fit depends on their goals, timeline, how long they plan to stay, and legacy priorities.
- **Defer** side-by-side comparison on their specific home to the LO on the call.

### Common HEI-related questions

| Lead asks… | What to know |
|------------|--------------|
| "Is this the same as home equity investment companies?" | No — different structures. HEI is equity-sharing; HECM is a federally insured loan program for retired homeowners. |
| "They said I don't have to take a loan." | HEI is structured as an investment, not a loan. Tradeoffs differ around equity retained, repayment timing, and long-term cost. |
| "What's the difference between HEI and reverse mortgage?" | HEI = sharing future home value. HECM = loan against equity with FHA insurance and non-recourse protections. |
| "I'm comparing options online." | Valid approach. Personalized comparison requires their specific home, equity, and goals — belongs on an educational call. |
