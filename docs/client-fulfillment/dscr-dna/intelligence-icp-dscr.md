### 1. Who Is This Person

Your ideal customer is **not** an anxious consumer shopping for relief. They are a **real-estate investor** — an operator and entrepreneur who treats property as a business. They think in numbers: cash flow, cap rate, cash-on-cash return, leverage, and velocity of capital. Their constraint is not fear; it is **trapped equity and bad financing** on rentals they already own — and a conventional refinance they can't get because of write-offs, property count, or income docs.

They are not buying a mortgage. They are buying **a better position on property they already own** — equity pulled out and redeployed, a lower or more stable payment, an exit from hard-money/balloon debt — all without W-2/DTI underwriting.

**Core Identity Snapshot**

| Category | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Profile | Active real-estate investor who **already owns** rentals (buy-and-hold, BRRRR, STR/Airbnb, small multifamily) |
| Age range | Broad (≈30–60); defined by activity, not age. No age targeting (business-purpose) |
| Income type | Self-employed, business owner, or investor with non-traditional / write-off-heavy income |
| Entity | Frequently holds / refinances in an **LLC / entity** for liability and tax structuring |
| Credit | Often strong (700+), but conventional DTI fails due to write-offs or property count |
| Constraint | Hit Fannie/Freddie 6–10 financed-property cap, or can't document income |
| Geographic fit | National; follows cash flow into secondary/tertiary markets |
| Sophistication | Medium–high; researches, compares lenders, runs the numbers before calling |

**Psychographic Profile**

| Dimension | Their internal reality |
|-----------|------------------------|
| Worldview | "Real estate is how I build wealth and replace my income. Speed and leverage win." |
| Core values | Cash flow, scalability, control, optionality, ROI, time |
| Relationship with money | Money is a tool to deploy, not protect. Comfortable with leverage and calculated risk. |
| Trust level | Skeptical of lenders who *don't get investors* — they fear slow closings and surprise conditions, not the product |
| Decision style | Analytical and fast — will move quickly on a lender who can actually close |
| Information sources | BiggerPockets, REIA meetups, investor YouTube/podcasts, LinkedIn, deal-analysis tools (PropStream, AirDNA) |
| Barriers to action | Rate/fee vs. conventional, "will it actually close," lender competence, does the deal pencil |

### 2. Before & After States

Marketing contrasts where the investor is **stuck** with where they want to be — but the axis is **trapped equity and bad financing on property they already own**, not emotional security.

**The 'Before' State — Their Current Friction**

- Want to refinance a rental but conventional underwriting stalls on tax returns / DTI.
- Equity is **trapped** in existing doors — they can't pull it out and redeploy it.
- Stuck in a **bridge / hard-money / balloon** loan that's coming due, with no clean exit.
- Self-employed income is "messy" on paper despite strong real cash flow.
- Sitting on a high rate or unstable structure that's eating their cash flow.
- Tired of re-documenting their whole financial life every time they refinance.

**The 'After' State — What They Want**

- Qualify the refinance on the **property's** cash flow, not their personal income.
- Pull trapped equity out (cash-out) and redeploy it — keep the flywheel turning.
- Refinance out of hard-money/balloon debt into a stable 30-year loan.
- Close the refi **fast**, in an **LLC**, without a property-count ceiling.
- A lender/partner who understands investors and closes when they say they will.

**The Golden Rule of DSCR Marketing:** They are not buying a loan. They are buying **a better position on property they already own** — equity unlocked, payment improved, bad debt escaped — without W-2 underwriting getting in the way. Sell the outcome (cash freed, payment fixed, no income docs), not the mechanism.

### 3. Friction → Impact

| Friction Source | What They Feel | How It Impacts Their Business |
|-----------------|----------------|-------------------------------|
| Conventional refi DTI rejection | "My tax returns make me look broke even though I cash-flow." | Refis die in underwriting; equity stays stuck |
| Trapped equity | "My money is locked in a property I already own." | Capital sits idle instead of compounding |
| Maturing bridge/hard-money/balloon | "My short-term loan is coming due and I have no exit." | Refinance pressure; risk of default or fire-sale |
| High rate / bad structure | "My payment is eating my cash flow." | Erodes returns on a property they already hold |
| Document fatigue | "I re-prove my whole life every time I refinance." | Friction tax on every refi |
| Lender who doesn't get investors | "I keep explaining DSCR to my loan officer." | Wasted time, surprise conditions, blown timelines |

### 4. What They're Really Buying

| What They Are NOT Buying | What They ARE Buying |
|--------------------------|----------------------|
| ❌ A loan | ✅ Equity unlocked from property they own |
| ❌ A mortgage product | ✅ A lower / more stable payment |
| ❌ Income-doc underwriting | ✅ Qualifying on the property, not their W-2 |
| ❌ A maturing hard-money/balloon note | ✅ A clean exit into a 30-year loan |
| ❌ Trapped equity | ✅ Capital velocity — pull out and redeploy |

### 6. The Primary Personas

One persona per campaign — same discipline as RM. (See objections in Section 7 and frames in [Doctrine DSCR Marketing](doctrine-dscr-marketing.md).)

**Persona 1: The Portfolio Scaler**
- Profile: Active buy-and-hold investor, 5–30 doors, sitting on equity across the portfolio.
- Constraint: Equity is trapped; conventional won't cash-out refinance because of property count.
- Core driver: Capital velocity — pull equity out and redeploy.
- Marketing angle: "No property-count ceiling. Cash out on each property's cash flow."
- Example hook: "Conventional capped you at 10. Your equity is just sitting there."

**Persona 2: The Self-Employed / Write-Off Investor**
- Profile: Business owner / self-employed; strong real cash flow, "messy" tax returns.
- Constraint: Write-offs sink a conventional refinance DTI despite genuine income.
- Core driver: Frustration with income-doc underwriting.
- Marketing angle: "Your tax strategy shouldn't block your refinance. Qualify on the property."
- Example hook: "Great at write-offs. Punished by DTI. Refinance on the property instead."

**Persona 3: The STR / Airbnb Operator**
- Profile: Short-term rental / vacation-rental operator; revenue-focused.
- Constraint: Conventional doesn't credit STR income well; needs revenue-based qualification.
- Core driver: Maximizing yield per door.
- Marketing angle: "Refinance your short-term rental on what it actually earns."
- Example hook: "Your Airbnb performs. Refinance it on the income, not your W-2."

**Persona 4: The Bridge / Hard-Money Refinancer**
- Profile: Investor who bought or rehabbed with a short-term loan (hard-money, bridge, balloon).
- Constraint: The short-term note is coming due; conventional refi is too slow or won't qualify.
- Core driver: A clean, certain exit before the maturity date.
- Marketing angle: "Refinance out of hard-money into a stable 30-year DSCR — before the balloon hits."
- Example hook: "Balloon coming due? Refinance into a 30-year loan that qualifies on the rent."

**Persona 5: The Foreign National Investor** *(program-dependent — confirm lender eligibility)*
- Profile: Non-US investor who **owns** US rental property; limited domestic credit/income history.
- Constraint: Conventional refinance underwriting requires domestic income/credit.
- Core driver: Accessing equity / better terms on US real estate they already hold.
- Marketing angle: "Refinance your US rental — qualify on the property, in an entity."
- Example hook: "No US tax returns? Refinance on the property's income."

### 7. Top 10 Objections & How to Address Them

| Objection | The Concern Behind It | How to Address It in Marketing |
|-----------|------------------------|--------------------------------|
| "The rate is higher than conventional." | Cost vs. alternatives | True — it's the trade for no income docs and entity vesting. Frame against the cost of *trapped equity* or a *maturing hard-money loan*, and run the numbers. |
| "Will it close before my balloon is due?" | Fear of a blown refi / maturity | Doc-light underwriting is built for speed; emphasize process and timeline (no guarantees — "in many cases"). |
| "Do I need to show my income/tax returns?" | Doc fatigue | No — qualification is the **property's rent vs. PITIA**, not personal income. |
| "Does my property even qualify?" | DSCR uncertainty | Walk the DSCR math (rent ÷ PITIA); show what ratio unlocks which terms (illustrative). |
| "Can I refinance in my LLC?" | Liability / structure | Most programs allow entity vesting; route tax/structuring specifics to their CPA/attorney. |
| "Conventional won't refinance me / I've hit my cap." | Blocked refi | DSCR qualifies on the property and generally has no conventional financed-property cap. |
| "How much equity do I need?" | Capital efficiency | Cash-out typically caps near ~75% LTV (illustrative); the refinance leaves equity in the property. |
| "I'm a foreign national." | Eligibility | Some programs accommodate foreign nationals refinancing US rentals (program-dependent — confirm). |
| "Interest-only sounds risky." | Payment shock | IO lowers payment now; model the reset — be transparent, no overpromising. |
| "How are you different from my bank?" | Lender competence | Investor-specialized: understands DSCR, entities, STR income, and refi speed — not a generalist conventional shop. |

### 8. AI Prompting Framework

Master template for generating on-brand DSCR assets. Fill the bracketed sections per campaign.

**Role & Goal:** You are a direct-response copywriter for **real-estate investors**. Generate copy that gets a qualified investor to request a DSCR **refinance** review on a rental they already own. Tone: peer-to-peer operator, numbers-literate, fast, credible — never the hand-holding, fear-based tone used for retirees.

**Product Framing:** A **business-purpose DSCR refinance** that qualifies on the property's rental cash flow, not personal income. **Refinance only — no purchase.** Unlike RM, **the product name is an asset** — investors search "DSCR loan." Lead with the investor outcome (equity unlocked, payment fixed, no income docs), then name the product.

**ICP snapshot (paste into every prompt):** Core identity: an active real-estate investor who already owns rentals and is constrained by trapped equity or bad financing they can't refinance conventionally. Core friction: [insert — e.g., conventional refi DTI rejection / trapped equity / maturing hard-money loan]. Desired outcome: [insert — e.g., cash out and redeploy, or exit a balloon into a 30-year loan]. What they're really buying: equity unlocked, a better payment, capital velocity.

**Ad copy structure:** (1) Hook — name the refinance friction. (2) Reframe — qualify on the property, not your income. (3) Solution — DSCR refinance mechanics in one line. (4) Proof — funded/closed outcome (substantiated). (5) CTA — "See if your property qualifies" / "Get a DSCR refinance quote."

**Negative constraints (always include):**
- Do NOT market for primary-residence or personal use (business-purpose only).
- Do NOT write purchase angles — the Waiz line is **refinance only**.
- Do NOT invent rates/LTV/DSCR numbers — use approved pricing only, framed as illustrative.
- Do NOT guarantee approval, rate, or closing timeline ("may qualify," not "you qualify").
- Do NOT give tax/legal advice — route entity/tax/depreciation to a CPA/attorney.
- Do NOT use age targeting or RM senior/relief framing.

### 9. Urgency & Tone

Unlike RM (where urgency backfires), investors respond to **deal-driven, real urgency** — but it must stay truthful and UDAP-compliant.

**Use (when true):** rate-environment context, **balloon / hard-money maturity** deadlines, "lock in terms before your short-term note is due." **Never:** fabricated scarcity, fake countdowns, guaranteed approvals/returns, or any deceptive net impression. See [DSCR Compliance Guardrails](dscr-compliance-guardrails.md).

**Themes:** DSCR refinance math walkthroughs; "conventional vs. DSCR refinance" comparisons; how to refinance with heavy write-offs; refinancing STR income; refinancing into an LLC (with CPA disclaimer); cash-out refi / BRRRR exit; escaping hard-money/balloon debt; rate/term refi case studies.

**Platforms:**

| Platform | Strategy |

| BiggerPockets / forums | Educational presence; answer DSCR questions; partner with investor creators. Highest-intent top-of-funnel. |

### 11. Quick Reference — Prompt Cheat Sheet

TARGET: Active real-estate investor who ALREADY OWNS rentals; self-employed / write-off-heavy / property-capped / stuck in hard-money or a high rate; often holds in an LLC; values speed, equity, control. SCOPE: REFINANCE ONLY — no purchase angles. THEY ARE BUYING: equity unlocked, a better payment, an exit from bad debt, qualifying on the property — NOT relief or security. PRODUCT NAME IS AN ASSET: say "DSCR loan" openly. ALWAYS: business-purpose/investment + refinance framing, "qualify on the property's cash flow," "no income docs," "refinance in your LLC," "may qualify." NEVER: invent pricing, guarantee approval/rate/timeline, primary-residence framing, purchase angles, age targeting, RM senior/relief tone, tax/legal advice. PERSONAS: Portfolio Scaler, Self-Employed/Write-off, STR Operator, Bridge/Hard-Money Refinancer, Foreign National (program-dependent).
