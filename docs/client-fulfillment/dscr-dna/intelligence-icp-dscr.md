### 1. Who Is This Person

Your ideal customer is **not** an anxious consumer shopping for relief. They are a **real-estate investor** — an operator and entrepreneur who treats property as a business. They think in numbers: cash flow, cap rate, cash-on-cash return, leverage, and velocity of capital. Their constraint is not fear; it is **access to capital fast enough to keep acquiring** when conventional financing taps out.

They are not buying a mortgage. They are buying **the ability to scale a portfolio** — more doors, faster closings, and freedom from W-2/DTI underwriting.

**Core Identity Snapshot**

| Category | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Profile | Active real-estate investor (buy-and-hold, BRRRR, STR/Airbnb, small multifamily) |
| Age range | Broad (≈30–60); defined by activity, not age. No age targeting (business-purpose) |
| Income type | Self-employed, business owner, or investor with non-traditional / write-off-heavy income |
| Entity | Frequently buys in an **LLC / entity** for liability and tax structuring |
| Credit | Often strong (700+), but conventional DTI fails due to write-offs or property count |
| Constraint | Hit Fannie/Freddie 6–10 financed-property cap, or can't document income |
| Geographic fit | National; follows cash flow into secondary/tertiary markets |
| Sophistication | Medium–high; researches, compares lenders, runs the numbers before calling |

**Psychographic Profile**

| Dimension | Their internal reality |
|-----------|------------------------|
| Worldview | "Real estate is how I build wealth and replace my income. Speed and leverage win." |
| Core values | Cash flow, scalability, control, optionality, ROI, time |
| Relationship with money | Money is a tool to deploy, not protect. Comfortable with leverage and calculated risk. |
| Trust level | Skeptical of lenders who *don't get investors* — they fear slow closings and surprise conditions, not the product |
| Decision style | Analytical and fast — will move quickly on a lender who can actually close |
| Information sources | BiggerPockets, REIA meetups, investor YouTube/podcasts, LinkedIn, deal-analysis tools (PropStream, AirDNA) |
| Barriers to action | Rate/fee vs. conventional, "will it actually close," lender competence, does the deal pencil |

### 2. Before & After States

Marketing contrasts where the investor is **stuck** with where they want to be — but the axis is **capital and scale**, not emotional security.

**The 'Before' State — Their Current Friction**

- Found a cash-flowing deal but conventional underwriting stalls on tax returns / DTI.
- Hit the financed-property cap — the bank won't finance the next one.
- Self-employed income is "messy" on paper despite strong real cash flow.
- A competing all-cash buyer or a 1031 clock is about to kill the deal on timing.
- Capital is trapped in equity they can't recycle into the next acquisition.
- Tired of re-documenting their whole financial life for every single property.

**The 'After' State — What They Want**

- Qualify on the **property's** cash flow, not their personal income.
- Close **fast**, in an **LLC**, and keep acquiring without a property-count ceiling.
- Recycle equity (cash-out) into the next deal — keep the flywheel turning.
- A repeatable financing path that scales with the portfolio.
- A lender/partner who understands investors and closes when they say they will.

**The Golden Rule of DSCR Marketing:** They are not buying a loan. They are buying **scale, speed, and leverage** — the ability to keep growing the portfolio without W-2 underwriting getting in the way. Sell the outcome (more doors, faster closings, no income docs), not the mechanism.

### 3. Friction → Impact

| Friction Source | What They Feel | How It Impacts Their Business |
|-----------------|----------------|-------------------------------|
| Conventional DTI rejection | "My tax returns make me look broke even though I cash-flow." | Deals die in underwriting; momentum stalls |
| Financed-property cap | "The bank won't finance my next one." | Hard ceiling on portfolio growth |
| Slow closings | "I'm going to lose this deal on timing." | Loses competitive/1031 deals to faster buyers |
| Document fatigue | "I re-prove my whole life every property." | Friction tax on every acquisition |
| Trapped equity | "My money is stuck in a property I already own." | Capital sits idle instead of compounding |
| Lender who doesn't get investors | "I keep explaining DSCR to my loan officer." | Wasted time, surprise conditions, blown timelines |

### 4. What They're Really Buying

| What They Are NOT Buying | What They ARE Buying |
|--------------------------|----------------------|
| ❌ A loan | ✅ Portfolio scale (more doors) |
| ❌ A mortgage product | ✅ Speed to close on the next deal |
| ❌ Income-doc underwriting | ✅ Qualifying on the property, not their W-2 |
| ❌ A property-count ceiling | ✅ A repeatable path that scales |
| ❌ Trapped equity | ✅ Capital velocity — recycle and redeploy |

### 6. The Primary Personas

One persona per campaign — same discipline as RM. (See objections in Section 7 and frames in [Doctrine DSCR Marketing](doctrine-dscr-marketing.md).)

**Persona 1: The Portfolio Scaler**
- Profile: Active buy-and-hold investor, 5–30 doors, building long-term rentals.
- Constraint: Hit the conventional financed-property cap; needs an alternative path.
- Core driver: Growth and velocity — keep acquiring.
- Marketing angle: "No property-count ceiling. Qualify on each property's cash flow."
- Example hook: "Conventional capped you at 10. Your portfolio isn't done."

**Persona 2: The Self-Employed / Write-Off Investor**
- Profile: Business owner / self-employed; strong real cash flow, "messy" tax returns.
- Constraint: Write-offs sink conventional DTI despite genuine income.
- Core driver: Frustration with income-doc underwriting.
- Marketing angle: "Your tax strategy shouldn't block your next deal. Qualify on the property."
- Example hook: "Great at write-offs. Punished by DTI. There's a better way to qualify."

**Persona 3: The STR / Airbnb Operator**
- Profile: Short-term rental / vacation-rental operator; revenue-focused.
- Constraint: Conventional doesn't credit STR income well; needs revenue-based qualification.
- Core driver: Maximizing yield per door.
- Marketing angle: "Finance the next short-term rental on its income."
- Example hook: "Your Airbnb performs. Finance the next one on what it actually earns."

**Persona 4: The Speed / 1031 Closer**
- Profile: Time-sensitive buyer — competitive offers or a 1031 exchange clock.
- Constraint: Conventional is too slow; deal dies on timing.
- Core driver: Certainty and speed to close.
- Marketing angle: "Close fast enough to win the deal — and beat the 1031 clock."
- Example hook: "45 days to identify, 180 to close. Financing that keeps up."

**Persona 5: The Foreign National Investor** *(program-dependent — confirm lender eligibility)*
- Profile: Non-US investor acquiring US rental property; limited domestic credit/income history.
- Constraint: Conventional underwriting requires domestic income/credit.
- Core driver: Access to US real estate as an asset class.
- Marketing angle: "Invest in US rental property — qualify on the property, in an entity."
- Example hook: "No US tax returns? Qualify on the property's income."

### 7. Top 10 Objections & How to Address Them

| Objection | The Concern Behind It | How to Address It in Marketing |
|-----------|------------------------|--------------------------------|
| "The rate is higher than conventional." | Cost vs. alternatives | True — it's the trade for no income docs, entity vesting, and no property cap. Frame as cost of *speed and scale*, and run the numbers on the deal. |
| "Will it actually close on time?" | Fear of a blown deal | Doc-light underwriting is built for speed; emphasize process and timeline (no guarantees — "in many cases"). |
| "Do I need to show my income/tax returns?" | Doc fatigue | No — qualification is the **property's rent vs. PITIA**, not personal income. |
| "Does my deal even qualify?" | DSCR uncertainty | Walk the DSCR math (rent ÷ PITIA); show what ratio unlocks which terms (illustrative). |
| "Can I close in my LLC?" | Liability / structure | Most programs allow entity vesting; route tax/structuring specifics to their CPA/attorney. |
| "I've hit my property cap." | Growth ceiling | DSCR generally has no conventional financed-property cap. |
| "Down payment is too high." | Capital efficiency | Higher down/reserves are the structure; frame against scale and cash-out redeploy. |
| "I'm a foreign national." | Eligibility | Some programs accommodate foreign nationals (program-dependent — confirm). |
| "Interest-only sounds risky." | Payment shock | IO lowers payment now; model the reset — be transparent, no overpromising. |
| "How are you different from my bank?" | Lender competence | Investor-specialized: understands DSCR, entities, STR income, and speed — not a generalist conventional shop. |

### 8. AI Prompting Framework

Master template for generating on-brand DSCR assets. Fill the bracketed sections per campaign.

**Role & Goal:** You are a direct-response copywriter for **real-estate investors**. Generate copy that gets a qualified investor to request DSCR financing info or a deal review. Tone: peer-to-peer operator, numbers-literate, fast, credible — never the hand-holding, fear-based tone used for retirees.

**Product Framing:** A **business-purpose DSCR loan** that qualifies on the property's rental cash flow, not personal income. Unlike RM, **the product name is an asset** — investors search "DSCR loan." Lead with the investor outcome (scale, speed, no income docs), then name the product.

**ICP snapshot (paste into every prompt):** Core identity: an active real-estate investor who treats property as a business and is constrained by access to fast capital. Core friction: [insert — e.g., conventional DTI rejection / property cap / slow closings]. Desired outcome: [insert — e.g., close fast in an LLC and keep acquiring]. What they're really buying: scale, speed, leverage, capital velocity.

**Ad copy structure:** (1) Hook — name the investor friction. (2) Reframe — qualify on the property, not your income. (3) Solution — DSCR mechanics in one line. (4) Proof — funded/closed outcome (substantiated). (5) CTA — "See if your deal qualifies" / "Get a DSCR quote."

**Negative constraints (always include):**
- Do NOT market for primary-residence or personal use (business-purpose only).
- Do NOT invent rates/LTV/DSCR numbers — use approved pricing only, framed as illustrative.
- Do NOT guarantee approval, rate, or closing timeline ("may qualify," not "you qualify").
- Do NOT give tax/legal advice — route entity/1031/depreciation to a CPA/attorney.
- Do NOT use age targeting or RM senior/relief framing.

### 9. Urgency & Tone

Unlike RM (where urgency backfires), investors respond to **deal-driven, real urgency** — but it must stay truthful and UDAP-compliant.

**Use (when true):** rate-environment context, deal/closing timelines, 1031 deadlines, "lock in terms on this acquisition." **Never:** fabricated scarcity, fake countdowns, guaranteed approvals/returns, or any deceptive net impression. See [DSCR Compliance Guardrails](dscr-compliance-guardrails.md).

### 10. Content Themes & Platforms

**Themes:** DSCR math walkthroughs; "conventional vs. DSCR" comparisons; how to qualify with write-offs; STR income financing; closing in an LLC (with CPA disclaimer); cash-out/BRRRR; speed/1031; portfolio scaling case studies.

**Platforms:**

| Platform | Strategy |

| YouTube / podcasts | Deal-analysis and DSCR education; investors learn here before they call. |

| Google Search | High-intent "DSCR loan" queries — lead with clarity, terms, and competence. |

### 11. Quick Reference — Prompt Cheat Sheet

TARGET: Active real-estate investor; self-employed / write-off-heavy / property-capped; often buys in an LLC; values speed, scale, leverage. THEY ARE BUYING: portfolio scale, speed to close, qualifying on the property — NOT relief or security. PRODUCT NAME IS AN ASSET: say "DSCR loan" openly. ALWAYS: business-purpose/investment framing, "qualify on the property's cash flow," "no income docs," "close in your LLC," "may qualify." NEVER: invent pricing, guarantee approval/rate/timeline, primary-residence framing, age targeting, RM senior/relief tone, tax/legal advice. PERSONAS: Portfolio Scaler, Self-Employed/Write-off, STR Operator, Speed/1031 Closer, Foreign National (program-dependent).
