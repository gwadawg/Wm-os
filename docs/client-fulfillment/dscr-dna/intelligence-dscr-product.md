### What It Is (Plain English)

A **Debt-Service-Coverage-Ratio (DSCR) loan** is an investment-property mortgage that qualifies on the **property's rental cash flow — not the borrower's personal income.** No W-2s, pay stubs, or tax returns. It is built for real-estate investors buying or refinancing income-producing property, typically as **business-purpose** financing and frequently closed in an **LLC/entity**. It is **not** a primary-residence loan.

### How Qualification Works

- **The ratio:** `DSCR = Gross Rental Income ÷ PITIA`, where **PITIA** = Principal + Interest + Taxes + Insurance + Association (HOA) dues.
- **What it means (illustrative):**
  - **Below 1.0** — rent does not fully cover the payment. Most lenders won't fund here; some specialty "no-ratio" / ~0.75 programs exist with higher down payment and rate.
  - **1.0** — break-even; rent exactly covers debt service.
  - **1.25+** — strong cushion; typically unlocks the best pricing tiers.
- **Income source:** market rent (appraiser 1007/1025) or actual lease — per lender program.

### What Investors Use DSCR For

- Acquiring the **next rental** when conventional caps out (Fannie/Freddie 6–10 financed properties).
- **Buy-and-hold** portfolio growth across SFR and small multifamily.
- **Short-term rental / Airbnb** acquisitions (some programs use STR revenue projections, e.g. AirDNA).
- **Cash-out refinance** to recycle equity into the next deal (BRRRR).
- **Fast-close** scenarios — including **1031 exchange** 45/180-day windows.
- Buying through an **entity** for liability protection and tax structuring (route specifics to their CPA/attorney).

### Where It Helps Most

- Self-employed investors whose tax returns show heavy write-offs that sink conventional DTI.
- Investors who have hit the conventional financed-property cap.
- STR operators and investors in secondary/tertiary cash-flow markets.
- Foreign nationals without domestic income/credit history (program-dependent).
- Investors who value **speed and low documentation friction** over the lowest possible rate.

### Quick Reality Checks

- DSCR rates run **higher** than conventional investment-property loans — the trade-off for no income docs and entity flexibility.
- The **property must cash-flow** (or come close) — a deal that doesn't pencil won't qualify at most lenders.
- It is **business-purpose / investment** financing — not for a primary residence. See [DSCR Compliance Guardrails](dscr-compliance-guardrails.md).
- Higher down payment and reserves than a typical primary-residence loan.

### Myths and Debunks

**Qualification**

- *Myth: I need to show my income / tax returns.* No — qualification is based on the **property's rent vs. PITIA**, not personal income. (Confirm program docs.)
- *Myth: My write-offs disqualify me.* The opposite advantage of DSCR — heavy write-offs that hurt conventional DTI are irrelevant when the property cash-flows.
- *Myth: I'm capped at 10 properties.* DSCR programs generally don't impose the conventional financed-property cap.

**Cost & Structure**

- *Myth: The rate is the same as a primary-residence loan.* DSCR is investment, business-purpose financing and prices higher; weigh it against speed, doc-light underwriting, and portfolio scale.
- *Myth: I can put 5% down.* Investment DSCR typically needs **~20–25%+ down**; there is no low-down primary-residence equivalent.
- *Myth: Interest-only is free money.* IO lowers the payment now but can create payment shock later; model it.

**Entity & Use**

- *Myth: I must close in my personal name.* Most DSCR programs **allow LLC/entity vesting** — common for liability and tax structuring (talk to your CPA/attorney).
- *Myth: I can use it for the house I live in.* No — owner-occupied is not eligible; DSCR is for **income-producing investment property**.

**Process**

- *Myth: It's slower than conventional.* Doc-light underwriting often closes **faster**, which is why 1031 and competitive-offer investors use it.
