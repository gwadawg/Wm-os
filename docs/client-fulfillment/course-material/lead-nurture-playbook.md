---

## The leaky bucket

You pay for clicks. Someone fills out your form. For a few minutes, their interest is at its **peak** — then life interrupts and they forget.

Ads fill the top of the bucket. Nurture plugs the holes. Without follow-up, most businesses stop after 1–3 touches; **most conversions happen between the 5th and 12th contact.**

Your job is not to "fix the ads." It's to **bridge the gap between form submit and booked conversation.**

---

## The four pillars

Think of four legs of a table — remove one and conversion collapses.

| Pillar | In plain language | Your role |
|--------|-------------------|-----------|
| **Availability** | Can they take the next step right now? | Keep your calendar open; confirm booking links work |
| **Speed** | Do you show up while they're still curious? | Waiz bot handles first minutes; **you** respond fast when they reply |
| **Personalization** | Does it feel like it's about *their* situation? | Approve LO voice; we match copy to what they asked for on the form |
| **Volume** | Do you give up too soon? | Trust the 90-day arc — silence ≠ "not interested" on Day 3 |

Full definitions and Waiz rules: [Lead Nurture Playbook](../client-marketing/playbook-lead-nurture.md#the-four-pillars).

## How Waiz runs your nurture

```text
Meta form → AI speed-to-lead (0–5 min) → 10-day education drip → you get a reply → AI books the call
                ↓ no reply yet
         Days 11–30 → Days 31–90 long-term nurture
```

You do **not** need to memorize every message. You **do** need to:

1. Show up prepared when a lead books (they've been educated — meet them there)
2. Reply personally if they text or email you directly mid-sequence
3. Keep your calendar and compliance disclosures current
