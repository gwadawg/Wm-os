# RM Borrower ICP — Client Education

> **Why this module exists:** Follow-up only works when you understand **who** you're following up with. Reverse mortgage borrowers are not shopping for a product — they're looking for relief. That changes how long they take to decide and how much trust you have to earn.

Full internal ICP reference (team only): [Intelligence ICP RM](../reverse-mortgage-dna/intelligence-icp-rm.md). This doc is the **client-safe teaching layer**.

---

## Who you're actually talking to

Your ideal borrower is typically a **house-rich, cash-poor retired homeowner** — often 62+, living on fixed income, with meaningful equity and real monthly pressure (bills, repairs, healthcare, debt).

They are usually **not** buying a "reverse mortgage." They're buying:

- Relief from monthly cash-flow stress
- Dignity and independence in the home they love
- A way to access equity without selling or taking on a new payment they can't afford

Say **retired homeowners** in client-facing copy — not age targeting. See [RM Compliance Guardrails](../reverse-mortgage-dna/rm-compliance-guardrails.md).

---

## The trust deficit (why they don't move fast)

Before they ever talk to you, most prospects carry decades of negative association with the **product name**. Media stories, scams, and word-of-mouth trained them to hear "reverse mortgage" as danger — not opportunity.

| What they feel | What it means for you |
|----------------|----------------------|
| Low trust in financial professionals | Education before pressure; outcome-first language |
| Fear of scams (especially from social/online leads) | Consistent identity, save-your-number, human assistant voice |
| Family skepticism | They may need time to talk to kids or spouse — silence ≠ no |
| "Debt frame" default | They hear "last resort," not "retirement tool" — nurture must reframe |

Doctrine detail: [Doctrine RM Marketing](../reverse-mortgage-dna/doctrine-rm-marketing.md) — trust deficit, debt frame vs retirement tool frame.

## How they decide (why cycles are longer)

| Trait | Typical borrower behavior |
|-------|---------------------------|
| **Decision style** | Slow, cautious — researches for **weeks or months** before acting |
| **Information sources** | TV, Facebook, peers, adult children — not one Google search |
| **Risk tolerance** | Very low — one bad impression can end the conversation |
| **D2C Meta leads** | Filled a form on impulse; interest peaks fast, then life interrupts |

**Implication for nurture:** A form fill is **interest**, not readiness. Your job is to stay present through the education arc until they're ready to book — not to close on touch one.

---

## Direct-to-consumer vs referral (why D2C is harder)

| Source | What the lead knows when you call |
|--------|-----------------------------------|
| **Referral / past client** | Who you are, some trust already transferred |
| **Meta / paid social (D2C)** | Saw an ad, clicked a form — may not remember your name or why they opted in |

D2C leads need **more** touches, not fewer:

1. Speed while motivation is high (first minutes matter)
2. Education that dismantles the debt frame over time
3. Volume — most competitors quit after one or two attempts
4. Personalization — match what they asked for on the form

---

## What "good follow-up" looks like for this ICP

- **Calm, educational tone** — never urgent, pitying, or salesy
- **Outcome-first** — payment relief, staying in the home, clearing debt — product name when teaching mechanics
- **Respect the timeline** — follow up like you mean it, but don't pressure a 90-day decision into a 90-minute close
- **Assume life interrupted them** — not that they rejected you

---

## Checklist — before you blame the leads

- [ ] I understand this borrower may need **5–12+ contacts** before booking
- [ ] I'm not treating silence on Day 2 as "bad lead"
- [ ] My team responds fast when they **reply** (hot lead)
- [ ] Copy and calls use outcome-first language, not product jargon on first touch
- [ ] I've read the [Nurture Framework](../client-marketing/playbook-nurture-framework.md) — why follow-up is sales
