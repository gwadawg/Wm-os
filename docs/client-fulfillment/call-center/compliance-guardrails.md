# Call Center — Compliance Guardrails

## Purpose

Keep every rep inside the same limits, on every offer, before a product script adds tighter rules.

## Scope

What a rep may explain, and what they hand to the client. Identity rules are in [Representation](representation.md). Product marketing compliance still applies on top of this page:

- Reverse mortgage: [RM Compliance Guardrails](../reverse-mortgage-dna/rm-compliance-guardrails.md)
- DSCR: [DSCR Compliance Guardrails](../dscr-dna/dscr-compliance-guardrails.md)

## The rep's depth

The rep may answer common questions about the product at a high level, enough for the lead to understand what the conversation is about.

The client breaks the product down. Depth, fit, and next steps belong on the client's call.

## Off limits

The rep does not discuss:

- Rates
- Quotes
- Costs
- Anything that states what the lead will pay or receive

The rep does not promise an outcome.

The rep does not tell the lead they qualify.

The rep does not tell the lead the client can definitely help them.

The rep may say the client is strong at this work and has helped other people in similar situations. That is experience. It is not a promise about this lead.

## When the rep does not know, or the question is off limits

Questions about rates, costs, or product detail past a high-level answer go to the client. The rep sets the call for that reason.

The exact deflection sentence is a script decision. Do not invent one here.

## The pressure point

Stage 5 of the [call framework](call-framework.md#5-pitch) is where this gets tested. Building the client's credibility pulls toward claims about what the lead will get. Breadth of options and past experience are fair. Anything that describes this lead's rate, cost, or result is not.
