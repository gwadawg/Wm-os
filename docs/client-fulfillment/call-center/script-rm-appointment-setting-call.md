# RM Call Script

> **DRAFT — first pass. Not approved for the floor.**
> Built on the [call framework](call-framework.md). Rules live in the [call center hub](README.md); this file is only the words. Where they disagree, the hub wins.

## Purpose

Word tracks for a reverse mortgage call, stage by stage, so a rep sounds like a competent human assistant instead of a reader.

## Scope

Homeowners who came in on an inquiry about tapping into their home equity. Front desk only — the rep books or transfers, the client explains the product.

## Before you dial

Open the client file. You need the client's name, title, company, the states they are licensed in, their hours, how many lenders and programs they work with, and whether they are approved for live transfer.

Open the lead's file. You need what they asked for and where the home is. You will say it back to them in stage 2.

## How to use this

The stages are fixed. The words are a starting point. Say them the way you talk — contractions, short sentences, real reactions. A rep reading this aloud word for word sounds worse than a rep who knows the stage they are in and says it their own way.

Brackets are things you fill from the file. Never guess one.

**Match their energy.** A soft, slow homeowner gets soft and slow. A loud guy with no time gets quick and direct. Same stages either way.

---

## Stage 1 — Introduction

> Hey — [FIRST NAME]?
>
> Hey [NAME], this is [REP] calling over from [CLIENT]'s office. How we doing?

Stop talking. Let them answer. One beat of being a human, then move.

> Good, good. Thanks for asking. So.. 

---

## Stage 2 — Context

Say back what they asked for and where the home is. Then ask if that is right.

> I saw here you filled out a form on our site to find out if you can potentially [Obective] home over there in [STATE] — is that right?

Match it to what they actually came in for:


| What they came in for           | Say it like this                                                                               |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Access equity / cash            | "looking at tapping into some of the equity in the home over in [STATE]"                       |
| Get rid of the mortgage payment | "looking at getting out from under that monthly mortgage payment on the house over in [STATE]" |
| More monthly breathing room     | "looking at freeing up a little more income each month using the home over in [STATE]"         |
| Pay off other debt              | "looking at using some of the equity in the house to clean up some other debt"                 |
| Work on the house               | "looking at pulling some equity out to get some work done on the house over in [STATE]"        |


You are after a yes. That yes is them confirming you are not a random dialer.

---

## Stage 3 — Reason for call

This is the frame. Do not skip it, and do not ask a single question before it.

> Yeah, so — just so you know, I'm actually [CLIENT]'s assistant. He's the [TITLE] over at [COMPANY]. He wanted me to reach out and see if we could help you out with what you're looking for — and if it makes sense, I'd love to just get you two introduced.

**If they ask who you're with:**

> Well my name is actually [Your Name], I'm [CLIENT]'s assistant — he's over at [COMPANY], he works with retired homeowners on their home equity options. You came through on an inquiry about [WHAT THEY CAME IN FOR] a little bit ago. Does that ring a bell?

---

## Stage 4 — Discovery

Open the file out loud. Reason first, then the question.

> Yeah, so i was actually just reading through the information you submitted and saw you're property's over there in [STATE], right? 

> Got it. And just to confirm, you put here that the property is worth something around [What file says].. Right?   
>
> Gotchya. I know you were looking to [Objective], now was there a specific time frame you were looking to get this done by? Or were you just trying to get more information? 

Stop at four or five. You are taking notes for the client, not underwriting.

**If they volunteer it all up front,** do not ask it again. Say it back:

> Perfect, yeah thats exactly what [Client Name] works with. 

**Listen for these.** They tell you which pitch to run in stage 5.


| What you hear                              | What it means                            |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| "I just want this mortgage payment gone"   | Payment pressure is the real driver      |
| "I'm on a fixed income"                    | Monthly breathing room                   |
| "I've got cards / medical bills piling up" | Debt sitting on top of a paid-down house |
| "The house needs work"                     | Equity for repairs                       |
| "I want to help my kids while I'm here"    | Family, not survival                     |
| "My husband passed" / "since I lost her"   | Slow down. Be a person first.            |


---

## Stage 5 — Pitch

Relevance, then proof, then credibility, then the ask.

> Perfect — I'm glad we're talking, honestly. What he offers is right up your alley.
>
>  He's helped a lot of people over in [STATE] in your exact spot: [SAY THEIR SITUATION BACK].

Then the piece that separates him from every other call they get:

> And the best part is, [CLIENT] literally specializes in helping retiree's with using their home equity. So he will be able look more in depth in your current situation and find a product that will help you acheive what you're looking for. .

Then the ask:

> So let's do this. Let me get you two introduced, he can walk you through what your options look like, and you can figure out the best path from there.

**Proof by situation.** Pick the one that matches what you heard.


| They said        | Say                                                                                                                               |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Payment pressure | "Getting homeowners out from under that monthly payment is a big part of what he does."                                           |
| Fixed income     | "A lot of the people he works with are on a fixed income and just want a little more room every month."                           |
| Other debt       | "He works with homeowners all the time who want to use the equity they've already built to clean up what's sitting on top of it." |
| House needs work | "Using equity to get work done on the house is one of the most common things he helps with."                                      |
| Helping family   | "That one comes up a lot — using what they've built to help family while they're still around to see it."                         |


**Do not say** what they'll qualify for, how much they can access, what it costs, or that this will work. Nobody on the phone knows that yet.

**Say no required monthly mortgage payment, and stop there.** Do not say "no payments." Homeowners still have obligations on the house, and explaining them is the client's job, not yours. See [Compliance guardrails](compliance-guardrails.md) and [RM Compliance Guardrails](../reverse-mortgage-dna/rm-compliance-guardrails.md).

## Stage 6 — Find time or transfer

**Live transfer** — only if the client file says approved, and you are inside their hours.

> I know I called you out of the blue here, but I can try to introduce you to him now if you've got a couple minutes to work with?
>
> Perfect, hang tight for me one sec, I'll put you on a quick hold so I can try him.

Hand-off language: [Live Transfer](script-live-transfer-warm-handoff.md).

**Booking**, when transfer is not on the table or they can't talk now:

> No worries at all. Let me get you on his calendar then. What's better for you — later today, or first thing tomorrow?

Go for the soonest thing they will actually keep.

**When the time is soft.** "You can try me at five and see if I'm around" is not a booking.

> Yeah, we could try you at five — but why don't we find a time that actually works for you? That way we don't have to go back and forth and waste your time.
>
> What do you say — you more of a morning person, or is later in the day better?

**Then ask what would stop them:**

> And is there any reason you wouldn't be able to make it at [TIME]? Just so we can plan around it.

If something comes up — an appointment, a grandkid, a ride they have to catch — move the time right there. That is the whole point of asking.

**Size the call:**

> And just so you know what you're walking into, it's like a 15-minute thing. You guys go over what you're trying to do, he'll tell you what the options look like.

---

## Stage 7 — Tie down and wrap

> Sweet — I've got you down for [TIME] [TIME ZONE].

> We'll be calling you from [GHL NUMBER]. Do me a favor and save it real quick — sometimes it comes up as spam on people and I don't want you missing him.

> You'll get a text confirmation here in a second too, and we'll shoot you a reminder before the call so it doesn't sneak up on you.

> And if something changes, just text that number back. I'll move it, no problem at all.

> Any last questions before I let you go? … Sweet. Have a good one, [NAME].

## Objections

Handle it, then go back to the stage you were in. Never answer and then float.

### "How much can I get?" / "What's this gonna cost me?"

> Honestly? I'm not licensed, so I'd just be guessing — and the last thing I want is to give you a number that turns out wrong. It depends on the home anyway. That's the whole reason I'm setting this up: he looks at your actual situation and gives you real numbers.

Then back to where you were.

### "Is this one of those reverse mortgages?"

Don't dodge it. Dodging is what makes it sound shady.

> Well that's one of the programs he works with — along with a few others. It just depends on your situation, and that's exactly what he'd go through with you. 

### "The bank's gonna take my house"

> I hear that one a lot, and I get why. All I'll say is you stay the owner of your home — that doesn't change. The rest of how it works is really his conversation, not mine, and he'll go through all of it with you. That's what the call is for.

### "My kids are gonna get stuck with the debt"

> That's usually the first thing people ask about, honestly — it's the family that matters. Your kids don't end up personally on the hook for it. [CLIENT] can walk you through exactly how that part works for your situation, because it's a big piece of what he explains.

### "I've heard horror stories" / "sounds too good to be true"

> Yeah, there's a lot of old stories floating around out there. Which is fair — I'd be skeptical too. That's honestly the reason the call exists. He'll show you how it actually works today, costs and all, and if it's not for you then it's not for you.

### "Is this a scam?"

> Haha, I don't blame you for asking since there's a lot of junk out there. But to answer, no, this isn't a scam — if it were, [CLIENT] and the whole company would lose their license. And to give you the peace of mind, I can send you all their information so you can look up their license and research before talking with them. Is that okay with you?

### "I'm getting blown up with calls" / "you people sold my info"

> Ah man, I hear that a lot. Let me just clear that up though — you came to us directly, through Facebook. We don't sell anyone's info, it's genuinely not in our interest. So whatever else you've got coming in, that's not associated with us.
>
> In fact this will be the last call, since I can introduce you to [CLIENT] directly.

Straight back into stage 3.

### "I'm busy right now"

> Totally fine, I'll get out of your hair. But real quick before I let you go, just so I understand — are you still looking at freeing up some of the equity in the house?

If yes, and they offer you a callback time — **do not take the callback.** They just gave you an open slot.

> I can definitely call you back at five. But actually — what if we just put five on the calendar with you and him instead? He can walk you through the options directly and you skip the middle step. Save you a conversation.

### "I need to think about it"

> You should think about it, honestly. That's the point of the call — he's not asking you to decide anything on it. You get the information, and then you think about it with the real picture instead of guessing. Takes about 15 minutes.

### "Just send me something in the mail / by email"

> Yeah, I can have him send something over. What's the best email? … And so he sends the right thing instead of a generic packet — what were you hoping to use the money for? 

Then go back for the time. Paper doesn't answer their actual question. He does.

### "My daughter handles this stuff for me"

> Oh, that's no problem, honestly — good to have someone in your corner on it. What's the best way to reach her? I'd love to get you both on with [CLIENT] at the same time so nobody's playing telephone.

Never treat this as a no. Get the name and the number.

### "I'm not interested anymore"

> Totally fair — can I ask what changed? Last we heard, you were looking at [WHAT THEY CAME IN FOR].

If it's timing, get a callback. If they genuinely handled it, close politely and let them go.

---

## Quality bar

- Assistant frame delivered before any discovery question
- The file read back to them out loud in stage 4
- Four or five questions, no underwriting, no numbers
- Their own situation repeated back in the pitch
- Live transfer attempted whenever the client is approved and in hours
- No soft times written down
- "Any reason you wouldn't make it" asked on every booking
- Number confirmed, save-it ask made, reminders mentioned
- Benefits, costs, and product depth routed to the client — never answered on the phone
- Scope breaches escalated, never silently dropped
