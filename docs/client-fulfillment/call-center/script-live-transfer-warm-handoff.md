# Call Center — Live Transfer

> **DRAFT.** Universal. Same hand-off for every client and every offer. The decision to attempt a transfer is in [Call flow](call-flow.md). This file is only the words once the lead has agreed to talk right now.

## Purpose

Get the lead and the client talking to each other, with the client already knowing what the lead wants, and the lead knowing who just joined.

## When to use

The client file says live transfer is approved, you are inside the client's hours, and the lead said they can talk now. If any of those is missing, book instead. See [Call flow](call-flow.md).

## How to use this

Three stages, in order. Stay on the line until both of them are talking. Then get off.

Brackets come from the call you just had and from the client file. Do not add numbers, rates, or a loan interview on this hand-off. High level is the point.

---

## Stage 1 — Put the lead on hold

Tell them they're going on a short hold, and why.

> [LEAD], hang tight for me one second. I'm gonna grab [CLIENT] real quick so I can introduce you two.

**Why it matters.** A hold with no reason feels like you dropped them. Naming the client and saying you're introducing them gives the wait a point, and it sets up stage 3 so the name isn't new.

Mute them, or put them on an actual hold. They should not hear the briefing.

---

## Stage 2 — Brief the client

Catch the client like a person. Then the situation, high level. Then tell them you're bringing the lead on.

> [CLIENT], what's going on? You free to talk?
>
> Okay, so I've got a lead here looking to get some help [WHAT THEY WANT — whatever they actually said]. [ONE OR TWO THINGS THEY TOLD YOU that change how the client should open — how many properties, that they're already talking to other loan officers, the state, the objective].
>
> Okay. I'm gonna introduce you right now. One second.

**Why it matters.** The client should open already knowing the situation, not by asking the lead to start over. Keep it to what the lead is trying to do and the one or two details that matter. A balance, a rate, and a home value turn this into a second discovery call the lead already did with you.

**If the client is not free,** do not merge the calls. Go back to the lead and book. See [Booking rules](booking-rules.md).

> Hey, sorry about that — he just got tied up. Let me get you on his calendar instead. What's better, later today or first thing tomorrow?

---

## Stage 3 — Introduce them

Come back to the lead first, so they know you didn't disappear. Then hand both names across, with the same situation you just told the client. Ask the client to take it. Leave once they do.

> [LEAD], you still there? Thanks so much for waiting.
>
> I've got [CLIENT] on the line here. He's the specialist I was just telling you about.
>
> [CLIENT], this is [LEAD]. He's looking to get some help [WHAT THEY WANT] over in [STATE]. Can you give him a hand?

Be sure to wait on the call to hear both of them communicate with each other before hanging up.



**Why it matters.** The lead hears a name they were already promised, and the title you used earlier, so this feels like a continuation instead of a new cold call. The client hears the same story you just briefed, so they can start helping instead of interviewing. You leave once that hand-off has landed. Staying on turns you into a third person in their conversation.
