# Call Center — Call Framework

## Purpose

The structure of a connected call, stage by stage, and why each stage exists. A rep who knows which stage they are in always knows what to do next.

## Scope

Every offer. The stages do not change by product. Word tracks do, and those are written in the script pass. Example lines on this page are illustration from training, not approved copy.

## The line

The call runs in one direction, start to finish. The value is not the words. It is knowing your position. When something pulls the call sideways, you handle it and return to the stage you left — you do not restart, and you do not skip ahead.

| # | Stage | What it does |
|---|-------|--------------|
| 1 | [Introduction](#1-introduction) | Earn the first few seconds |
| 2 | [Context](#2-context) | Show why we have their information |
| 3 | [Reason for call](#3-reason-for-call) | Set identity and frame the outcome |
| 4 | [Discovery](#4-discovery) | Confirm the file, find the objective |
| 5 | [Pitch](#5-pitch) | Tie their objective to this client, then ask |
| 6 | [Find time or transfer](#6-find-time-or-transfer) | Get a conversation that will actually happen |
| 7 | [Tie down and wrap](#7-tie-down-and-wrap) | Protect the show |

## 1. Introduction

Name, who you are, where you are calling from, and a question that gets them talking.

Use the lead's first name as a question. It confirms you reached the right person and it makes them answer rather than listen. State that you are calling from the client's office, per [Representation](representation.md).

**Why it matters:** this is a cold pickup. You have a few seconds before they decide you are a telemarketer. A name and a warm question buys the rest of the call.

## 2. Context

Say back what they asked for and where the property is, then ask if that is right.

> *Illustration:* "I saw you're looking to take cash out on your rental property there in California — is that right?"

**Why it matters:** two jobs at once. It explains how you have their details, and it gets a yes. The lead stops wondering who you are, because a stranger would not know this. It is the first piece of the legitimacy work described in [Show rate on the call](show-rate.md).

## 3. Reason for call

You are the client's assistant. Name the client, name their title and company, say they asked you to reach out, and frame the destination as a conversation worth having.

> *Illustration:* "I'm Amir's assistant. He's the branch manager at Westcap. He wanted me to reach out and see if we could help with what you're looking for — and if it makes sense, introduce you both."

**Why it matters:** this is the frame for the whole call. It installs the assistant identity before any questions, so the lead never thinks they are talking to the expert and never tells their whole story to the wrong person. It also sets the ask low: see if it makes sense to meet, not buy anything.

Facts about the client come from the client file only. See [Representation](representation.md).

## 4. Discovery

Confirm what is on file, then find the objective.

Open the file out loud, so they hear that this is a record and not a cold list. Soften each question with a reason for asking.

> *Illustration:* "Just so I have clarity, I'm looking through your file here — the property is in Texas, right? Is that your primary residence?" … "Just so I understand, given the home is worth X, how much were you looking to take out?" … "What's the objective — anything specific you're trying to get done, or taking a look at options?"

**Why it matters:** two different jobs. Confirming the file proves you are who you say you are. Finding the objective gives you the material for stage 5 — without it, the pitch is generic and the appointment is weak. This is the interest-building work in [Show rate on the call](show-rate.md).

**The one stage you compress.** If the lead volunteers their situation early, do not re-ask it. Reflect it back, confirm it, and move on. Asking questions they already answered costs you credibility.

## 5. Pitch

Take what they just told you, point it at this client, and ask.

Three pieces, in order:

1. **Relevance.** Their objective is exactly the thing this client does.
2. **Proof.** This client has done it for people like them — same type of borrower, ideally the same state.
3. **Credibility.** What makes the client able to help. Title, company, and breadth of options.

> *Illustration:* "I'm glad we're talking, this is right up your alley. Amir has helped dozens of investors in Texas with that exact thing." … "Since he's the branch manager at Westcap, he has access to over a hundred different lenders."

**Why it matters:** the lead agrees to the meeting because of fit, not because you asked nicely. Specific proof beats general praise — "investors in Texas doing a cash-out" lands where "he's great" does not.

**Tonality carries this stage.** The credibility line is where emphasis does the work. Said flat, "access to a hundred lenders" is a detail. Said with weight, it is the reason to take the call. Find the words that deserve stress and hit them.

**Stay inside the guardrails.** Breadth of options and experience are fair. Rates, costs, and what this lead will get are not. See [Compliance guardrails](compliance-guardrails.md).

## 6. Find time or transfer

If the client is approved for live transfer and you are inside their hours, go for right now. Otherwise, open the calendar and get a time.

> *Illustration:* "I've got his calendar up — are you free later today?"

**Why it matters:** this is the conversion. The decision order between transfer and booking is in [Call flow](call-flow.md), and what counts as a real commitment is in [Booking rules](booking-rules.md).

**Weigh out a soft time.** When the lead says you can try them at five and see if they are around, do not write it down. Agree with the fact, then redirect to a time they will keep, and give a reason that respects both sides.

> *Illustration:* "Yeah, we could try at five — but why don't we find a time that actually works best for you? He's a busy guy, and I don't want to waste your time or his."

**Then ask what would stop them.** "Is there any reason you wouldn't be able to make it at five?" surfaces the real conflict — a school pickup, a shift, a commute — and lets you move the time before it becomes a no-show instead of after.

**Set the size of the call.** Around fifteen minutes, going over goals, nothing to decide. A lead who thinks they are walking into a sales close finds a reason to be busy.

## 7. Tie down and wrap

The appointment exists. Now protect it.

1. **Confirm the number** the call will come from.
2. **Ask them to save it.** Tell them why: the number sometimes shows up as spam, and you want them to recognize it. This is the conversational half of the number problem. Rotating a flagged number is the operational half, in [Dial cadence](dial-cadence.md).
3. **Tell them reminders are coming**, so the messages are expected rather than suspicious.
4. **Offer the reschedule.** Invite them to tell you if the time stops working. A lead who reschedules is kept. A lead who feels locked in ghosts.
5. **Restate the time with the time zone.**
6. **Ask for last questions**, then close it out.

**Why it matters:** everything here is show-rate insurance and costs you thirty seconds. The reminder sequence that follows is a separate layer, listed in the [call center hub](README.md).

## Objections: handle, then return

An objection is not a detour off the line. It is a stop on it.

Never brush past one. Answer it directly, then step back to the exact stage you were in and continue. The return is what makes it work — handling an objection and then floating with no destination loses the call.

**Pattern: "I get blown up with calls / you people sell my information."** Sympathize honestly, separate yourself with facts, then return to the reason for the call. The facts available to you are that they contacted us directly through Facebook, that we do not sell their information, and how many times we have tried to reach them. The pivot back is the assistant frame from stage 3.

**Pattern: "I'm busy right now."** Release the pressure first — offer to get out of their hair. Then still deliver the reason for the call, because they will not remember you otherwise. Then check that the interest is live.

**Do not accept a callback for yourself.** When a lead offers you a time to call them back, that is an open slot they have already agreed to. Convert it into the appointment with the client instead of a second conversation with you.

> *Illustration:* "Instead of me calling at five — what if we just book five with you and Amir? He can walk you through the options directly, and you skip a step."

A lead who says they are busy can almost always give you one more minute. Ask for it.
