### Purpose & Scope

What this document is and how it should be used

This document defines the three phases every client campaign moves through after onboarding. Each phase has distinct performance characteristics, expected constraints, and normalized behaviors. It exists to answer one question:

### Corequestion

Is what this client is experiencing normal for the phase they are currently in — or is it a genuine system failure requiring intervention?

PRIMARY CONSUMER: This document is written for Manus (AI agent) and the Client Success team. When a client raises a concern, flags slow results, or questions the system, the first step is to identify which phase they are in and cross-reference the concern against the expected behavior for that phase.

It is not a client-facing document. Do not share it directly with clients.

### Phase 1

Testing

Weeks 1 – 4  |  Data collection before optimization is possible

What Is Happening

The ad budget is distributed across multiple ad sets simultaneously — different creative, different audiences, different angles. No single combination has been proven to win yet. The system is collecting real-world data on what resonates with this audience in this market at this time. This is not a delay. It is the work. Skipping or abandoning this phase means the Optimization phase never arrives.

What Is Normal in This Phase

NORMAL — Do Not Escalate

ABNORMAL — Investigate

Lead volume is low or inconsistent. Budget is spread across test sets.

Zero leads after 14+ days with confirmed ad spend running.

Higher-than-benchmark CPL. Optimization has not yet occurred.

Ad account restricted or campaigns rejected repeatedly.

No closed deals yet. Pipeline is building, not yet producing.

CRM not receiving leads. Integration appears broken.

Some leads not responding or no-showing. Contact rate will improve as sequence matures.

Reverse Sales Assistant has not contacted any new leads within 24 hours of opt-in.

Client feels uncertain or questions if the system is working. This is expected at this stage.

Client has not completed onboarding training and is actively working leads without proper setup.

How to Handle Client Concerns in This Phase

When a Phase 1 client expresses concern about results, the CSM response framework is:

### Step 1

Confirm they are in Phase 1 — verify campaign launch date and current week.

### Step 2

Validate the concern against the Normal/Abnormal table above. If it is listed as Normal, communicate that directly with confidence.

### Step 3

Redirect the client to their job in this phase: Complete training modules. Set up CRM. Get the follow-up process ready.

### Step 4

If anything in the Abnormal column is confirmed — create a ClickUp ticket and escalate to Ops immediately.

Key Constraint for AI Reference

### Doctrine

ROI cannot and should not be evaluated in Phase 1. A client who reports no closed deals in Week 2 is on schedule, not behind. Calibrate all AI-generated responses and assessments to this phase constraint.

### Phase 2

Optimization

Weeks 4 – 8  |  Winning combinations identified, budget concentrating

What Is Happening

The testing data has surfaced winning ad sets. Budget is actively shifting toward the combinations that produced the best results. Lead cost begins to drop. Volume increases. The pipeline starts to fill with real, qualified conversations. This is where most clients begin to see tangible momentum for the first time.

What Is Normal in This Phase

NORMAL — Do Not Escalate

ABNORMAL — Investigate

Some leads still unresponsive. Re-engagement sequences are still warming.

Lead cost not declining after 6+ weeks of active campaign. Winning ad sets have not emerged.

First closed deals may or may not have occurred yet. Early closings are a bonus, not a benchmark.

No appointments being booked at all. Reverse Sales Assistant outreach is not converting.

Some no-shows. Show rate typically below final target during this phase.

Show rate is 0% across 20+ booked appointments. Possible booking or reminder automation failure.

Client still building familiarity with CRM workflow and disposition process.

Client is not updating dispositions after calls. Data integrity is being lost.

Volume is increasing but inconsistent week-over-week. Optimization is not yet complete.

Volume is declining compared to Phase 1. Budget concentrated but performance regressing.

Key Constraint for AI Reference

### Doctrine

Phase 2 is the most emotionally volatile phase for clients. They have invested for 4–8 weeks and are beginning to expect returns. The correct response is to point to the improving cost trend and pipeline depth — not to validate impatience. If the optimization signals are present (declining CPL, rising volume), the system is working correctly.

### Phase 3

Compounding

Week 8+  |  Calibrated system — volume, consistency, and layered returns

What Is Happening

The system is calibrated. Lead costs are lower, volume is higher, and the re-engagement sequences are now firing on leads from weeks prior. Referrals begin layering on top of paid leads. This is the compounding phase — every prior input is now producing layered output. The pipeline has depth. Closings become more consistent.

What Is Normal in This Phase

NORMAL — Do Not Escalate

ABNORMAL — Investigate

Week-over-week volume variation of 10–20%. Normal campaign oscillation.

Volume drops more than 30% week-over-week without a budget change or ad account event.

Re-engagement sequences surfacing older leads who previously did not respond.

Re-engagement not firing. AI follow-up sequences appear inactive.

Some clients hit a plateau and resist scaling ad spend. This is a behavioral constraint, not a system failure.

CPL trending upward over 3+ consecutive weeks despite stable creative and budget.

Client asks about budget increases. This is the correct question at this phase.

Appointment show rate declining significantly from Phase 2 baseline without explanation.

Key Constraint for AI Reference

### Doctrine

Phase 3 concerns are almost always either behavioral (client not scaling, not working leads consistently) or genuine system issues (integration failure, declining ad performance). Separate these clearly before routing to the correct response. Behavioral issues are handled by CSM coaching. System issues are escalated to Ops.

### Ai Agent Reference Rules

Binding rules for Manus when processing client performance inquiries

### Rule 1

Always identify the client's current phase before evaluating any performance concern. Phase context is required for all assessments.

### Rule 2

Never evaluate ROI in Phase 1. If a client or team member asks for ROI assessment during the Testing phase, acknowledge the question and redirect to phase-appropriate benchmarks.

### Rule 3

Distinguish between behavioral failure (client not executing their role) and system failure (Waiz infrastructure breaking). These route to CSM coaching vs. Ops escalation respectively.

### Rule 4

When generating client-facing language about performance, always frame concerns within the phase context. A slow Phase 1 is not a broken system. A slow Phase 3 may be.

### Rule 5

When in doubt about phase identification, use campaign launch date plus current date to calculate week number. Weeks 1–4 = Phase 1. Weeks 4–8 = Phase 2. Week 8+ = Phase 3.
