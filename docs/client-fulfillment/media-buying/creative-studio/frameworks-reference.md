## §F1 — DR copy doctrine (distilled, self-contained)

Distilled from Waiz's direct-response copy doctrine. Principle: copy is persuasion
engineering — every block must have a job, every word must defend its place.

**The 5 strategic questions** (answer before writing any concept):

1. Who is the audience and what pain do they *verbalize*? (their words, not ours)
2. What is the real desired result/emotion underneath the stated pain?
3. What is the root cause they are *not seeing*? (the unique problem)
4. What is the specific solution/mechanism that attacks that root cause?
5. What proof makes the promise believable *now*?

**Mechanism standard.** A promise without a mechanism sounds false. A good mechanism:
explains why past attempts failed, names/shapes the root cause, connects directly to the
solution, is explainable in one sentence, and can't be copied by a competitor without adapting.

**Proof standard.** Weak proof = "lots of people got results." Strong proof = name, number,
context, before/after, a verifiable detail, and the objection it destroys.

**Framework composition.** Max 3 lenses per asset: one structure lens, one emotion lens,
one mechanism lens. Never use a framework as a fill-in form — absorb its job and write for
*this* audience, offer, and channel.

**Quality escalation** (when a draft is weak):
- *Too generic* → swap abstract words for VOC; add a proprietary detail (mechanism, proof, story, data); if still weak, redo the root-cause/solution thinking.
- *Flat emotion* → reopen the underlying fear/desire the audience won't say out loud; write a concrete scene, not adjectives.
- *Low credibility* → strengthen proof; explain the mechanism; reduce the promise or mark the gap `[TO FILL]`.

**Final test** for every block: Why does this block exist? Why these words? Why this order?
Why this tone? Would the avatar keep reading? If you can't answer, the block is inertia — rewrite it.

## The awareness bridge (one language)

The DR doctrine (F1) reasons in Eugene Schwartz's 5 awareness levels; the playbook reasons in
TOF/MOF/BOF. The studio treats them as one spectrum:

| Schwartz awareness (F1) | Funnel stage (F5) | Job of the ad | Product name? |
|-------------------------|-------------------|---------------|---------------|
| Unaware / Problem-aware | **TOF** | Break pattern, name invisible pain, shift Debt -> Retirement-Tool frame | Never |
| Solution-aware / Product-aware | **MOF** | Differentiate mechanism, bust myths, handle objections | In context only, never as opener |
| Most-aware | **BOF** | Prove superiority, position the LO, reduce risk, convert | Yes, directly |

## §F7 — Hook taxonomy (RM-adapted)

The 10 hook types the ideation matrix and script generator draw from. Each is mapped to the
awareness level where it lands best and screened against compliance (F6).

| Hook type | Best awareness/stage | RM example direction | Compliance watch |
|-----------|---------------------|----------------------|------------------|
| Rhetorical question | TOF | "Why does your home being worth more leave you with less?" | No product name |
| Confessional / first-person | TOF | "The last thing I want is for my kids to take care of me." | Dignity, not pity |
| Specific pain | TOF | "Choosing between groceries and the electric bill at 72." | No age in copy ("retired homeowners") |
| Demonstration | MOF | "Here is exactly how the equity becomes monthly income." | No guaranteed amounts |
| Myth-bust / shock | MOF | "No, the bank does not take your home." | Must be factually true |
| Authority | MOF/BOF | "After helping [X] homeowners in [State]..." | No fabricated stats |
| Curiosity gap | TOF | "Most retired homeowners never learn this about their house." | No clickbait falsehood |
| Social proof | MOF/BOF | "I just wish we'd done this sooner." | Real or clearly hypothetical |
| Niche callout | TOF/MOF | "Retired homeowners in [State]:" | No protected-class exclusion |
| Paradox / contrast | TOF | "House-rich, cash-poor — and it doesn't have to stay that way." | No false promise |

**DNA-of-winner (5 layers).** When studying a proven ad, separate: (1) the *hook* mechanic,
(2) the *emotional driver*, (3) the *frame/angle*, (4) the *proof structure*, (5) the *CTA + format*.
Reuse the layer, not the wording.

**5 genuine-variation methods** (for the `vary` command): same hook / different body; same
body / different hook; same copy / different talent or format; same structure / different angle;
same concept / different awareness stage. Never fake variations (ratio/color/emoji swaps).

## Mental-model map (F2, where each applies)

| Need in an RM ad | Mental model (F2) | Where the studio uses it |
|------------------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| Stop the scroll | Pattern interrupt, Curiosity (Zeigarnik open loop) | Hook beat |
| Make them feel understood | Liking/Similarity, Confirmation bias (align with belief) | Empathy beat |
| Shift the frame | Framing effect, Contrast effect (before/after) | Frame Shift beat |
| Lower risk / fear | Loss Aversion (reframed), Authority, Social Proof | Proof beat |
| Move to action calmly | Commitment & Consistency (small step), low-friction CTA | CTA beat |
| Ethical urgency only | Scarcity (genuine, advisor-framed per ICP §9) | CTA beat (optional) |

## How outputs cite this doc

- Ideation matrix tags each concept with its hook type (F7) and awareness/stage (F1/F5).
- Script generator attaches a **Frameworks Applied** block naming the models used per beat,
  e.g. `Hook: confessional (F7) + open loop (F2 Zeigarnik); Proof: Loss Aversion (F2) + FHA reassurance (F4)`.
- The compliance gate ([compliance-gate-checklist.md](compliance-gate-checklist.md)) enforces F6 + the DR quality rule (F1 §F1).
