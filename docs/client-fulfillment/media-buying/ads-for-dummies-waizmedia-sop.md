Facebook Setup - Understanding Assets

This guide explains both the main setup scenarios and the finer points of Business Portfolio Access for seamless asset sharing.

How to Find the Business ID

Navigate to business.facebook.com and log in.

Business ID Location:

It’s visible in the URL when you are inside the Business Manager.

Example: https://business.facebook.com/settings/business/1234567890

Business ID in this case: 1234567890

Alternatively, navigate to Business Settings, and the ID will be displayed under Business Info.

Pro Tip: If you're ever unsure, always check the URL for the Business ID—it’s the fastest way!

Requesting Business Portfolio Access from Partners

Use this when you need full control over assets like ad accounts or Facebook pages.

Steps to Request Access:

Log in to Facebook Business Manager.

Go to Business Settings from the main menu on the left.

Select Partners from the left sidebar.

Click Add.

Choose: “Request Access to a Business Portfolio.”

Enter the Client’s Business Portfolio ID (provided by the client).

Set the Access Level: Select Full Control.

Submit the Request: Click Submit to send your request for the client’s approval.

How Clients Can Approve Business Portfolio Access

Follow these steps to help the client approve your request for Business Portfolio access.

Steps for the Client to Approve:

Log in to Facebook Business Manager.

Go to Business Settings from the left-hand sidebar.

Select Partners from the menu.

Look for Pending Requests under the Access Requests section.

Find the Request: Locate your specific request for Business Portfolio access.

Review the Request Details, including the level of access requested.

Click Approve to grant access.

Receive Confirmation: After approval, you’ll get a notification confirming that access has been granted.

Why This Matters: Understanding Assets and Roles

Ad Accounts: Control who can create and manage campaigns.

Facebook Pages: Assign admins and editors for content management.

Business Portfolio: A collection of all assets (Pages, Ad Accounts, Pixels, Catalogs) under a Business Manager.

Partner Access: Enables one Business Manager to access another's assets with set permissions.

Handling Facebook Account Restrictions and Payment Issues

This SOP outlines how to manage Facebook account restrictions, such as payment method issues, content violations, or other compliance problems. It also details how to handle verification code requests and ensure that the Media Buyer coordinates with the Client Success Manager (CSM) to work directly with clients for resolution.

Types of Facebook Restrictions and Resolution Steps

1. Payment Method Issues

When Facebook restricts an ad account due to payment failures, this usually means the payment method on file has expired, been declined, or there are outstanding payments.

Steps for Media Buyer and Client Success Team

Media Buyer Actions:

Pause active campaigns to prevent further issues until resolved.

Notify the CSM immediately about the restriction through monday.com and submit a ticket to them.

CSM to Client:

Reach out to the client to explain the issue.

Schedule a call with the client to walk them through payment updates if needed.

Client Action (On Call):

Log in to Business Manager through the link pinned at the top of the client management on monday.com

Navigate to Payment Settings:

Go to Business Settings > Payments.

Update the Payment Method:

Add a new card or ensure the existing one is valid.

Clear Any Outstanding Balance:

Make sure all outstanding invoices are paid.

Verify Billing Information:

Confirm that the billing address matches the card on file.

Next Steps:

Media Buyer monitors to see if the restriction lifts.

If issues persist, escalate to Facebook Support through the Help Center.

2. Content Violations

Facebook may restrict accounts if an ad, post, or page violates community standards or ad policies.

Steps for Media Buyer and CSM Coordination

Media Buyer Actions:

Log in to Business Manager through the link pinned at the top of the client management on monday.com

Review Policy Violations:

Navigate to Account Quality > Policy Violations.

Submit an Appeal (if applicable):

Click on the specific violation and select Request Review if the content complies with policies.

Remove or Edit Violating Content:

Delete or update the offending content to comply with Facebook’s standards.

Next Steps:

Media Buyer will monitor the account status and provide updates to the CSM and the client.

If necessary, escalate to Facebook Support through the Help Center

Worst case scenario: Transition ads to a new ad account ( this a entire different topic and it’s out of ad for dummies guide )

3. Restricted Due to Account Security or Verification Requirements

Sometimes Facebook restricts accounts for security concerns, such as missing verification or suspicious activity.

Steps for Media Buyer and CSM Coordination

Media Buyer Actions:

Identify the reason for the restriction: Check notifications and Account Quality in Business Manager.

Notify the CSM about the restriction and submit a ticket.

CSM to Client:

Schedule a call with the client to complete any required verification steps.

Client Action (On Call):

Log in to Business Manager through the link pinned at the top of the client management on monday.com

Navigate to Security Center:

Go to Business Settings > Security Center if prompted for security checks.

Upload Necessary Documents:

If Facebook requests ID verification or business documents, the client will need to upload them through the portal.

Handle Verification Codes:

If Facebook sends a verification code to the client’s phone, the CSM or Media Buyer must:

Call the client directly to retrieve the code.

Enter the code promptly in Facebook’s verification field.

Note: Verification codes are time-sensitive, so ensure the client is available on the call.

Next Steps:

Monitor the account for status updates.

Escalate to Facebook Support if verification issues persist.

Meta Advertising Policies for the Beauty and Healthcare Industries

Meta’s advertising policies aim to create a safe and positive experience for users. Advertisers in the beauty and healthcare industries must adhere to these specific guidelines to prevent ad disapproval and account restrictions.

1. Prohibited Content

Health-Related Claims

No Unsubstantiated Health Claims: Advertisements must not make misleading or exaggerated claims about health benefits. For example, avoid stating that a product can cure, prevent, or treat a disease without robust scientific evidence.

Prohibition of Specific Drugs and Treatments: Certain medications, including prescription drugs like semaglutide, cannot be mentioned directly in advertisements unless the advertiser is authorized to do so.

Before and After Images

Prohibition of Before-and-After Photos: Ads that showcase before-and-after images to demonstrate the effectiveness of beauty or healthcare products (e.g., cosmetic treatments, weight loss) are generally not permitted. These images can be misleading and set unrealistic expectations.

Personal Health Information

No Personal Testimonials: Avoid using personal testimonials that claim specific results, especially if they can be interpreted as guarantees of performance. Ads should not use phrases like “I lost X pounds” or “This product changed my life.”

Sensitive Topics

Prohibition on Sensitive Topics: Advertisements should not discuss sensitive health issues, including mental health conditions, substance abuse, or eating disorders, in a way that could exploit the audience's vulnerabilities.

2. Restricted Content

Cosmetic Procedures and Treatments

Cosmetic Procedures: Ads promoting cosmetic procedures (e.g., Botox, fillers, surgeries) must be targeted only to adults. They should include clear information about potential risks, benefits, and side effects.

Weight Loss Products

Weight Loss Products: Ads promoting weight loss must avoid unrealistic results and ensure that claims are substantiated. Avoid images that could imply instant results or unhealthy body standards.

Supplements and Herbal Products

Claims About Supplements: Advertisements for dietary supplements must be based on credible scientific evidence, and claims should not imply that they can replace a balanced diet or lead to significant health benefits without proof.

3. Targeting Restrictions

Age Restrictions: Ads for certain beauty and healthcare products must be targeted only to users over a specific age (typically 18+), especially when the product is related to cosmetic procedures or sensitive health topics.

Geographic Restrictions: Some products may be subject to local regulations that prohibit advertising in certain areas. Advertisers must ensure they comply with these regional restrictions.

4. Transparency and Disclosure

Clear and Honest Advertising: All ads must clearly represent the product or service being offered. Any limitations or conditions should be disclosed upfront to avoid misleading the audience.

Privacy Policy: Advertisers should provide a clear privacy policy regarding the use of personal health information if applicable.

Consequences of Non-Compliance

Ad Rejection: Ads that violate Meta’s advertising policies will be rejected.

Account Suspension: Repeated violations can result in the suspension or permanent ban of advertising accounts.

Reputation Damage: Non-compliance can lead to reputational damage and decreased trust among potential customers.
