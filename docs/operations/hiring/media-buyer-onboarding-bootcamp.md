## So what does the onboarding/ramp up process look like? (Miro board copy)
## Day 0: Offer Sent & Contract Signed
The moment the contract is signed, we immediately start onboarding the new team member following our Media Buyer Initial Onboarding Steps SOP to get them access to everything and to get their onboarding call booked.

## Day 1: Onboarding Call
This is a quick 30-minute call to welcome them to the team, set expectations, and walk them through the initial steps. You'll follow the Media Buyer Onboarding Call SOP for this.

## Day 1-2: Company Trainings & Media Buying Fundamentals
The media buyer goes through the Entire Team Module (vision, values, mission, core team training, etc.) and the Fundamentals Folder in the Media Buyer Bootcamp (the 5-10 core videos on the basics of their role). This is the foundational knowledge they need before they can touch a client campaign.

## Day 3: Fundamentals & Setup Assessment Call with Manager
After completing the fundamentals, they have an Assessment Call with you to validate they've understood everything. If you're more than a 7/10 on their ability, move them to start setting up test campaigns; if not, have them re-go through the training again.

## Day 4-6: Setup & Serve Review Call with Manager
Go through campaign setup & management trainings then complete the following:

Setup a test client campaign and get feedback from your manager (Loom record entire setup or on call)

Go through 5 client accounts & note down recommended changes on a google doc & get manager feedback

## Day 7: 1-1 Work Manager
## Audit client campaign they setup & give feedback
## Audit recommended changes to 5 campaigns doc they made
## Focus this call on coaching and answering any relevant questions the MB may have
## Day 8-10: Audit performance calls & shadow
Listen to 3 client performance call reviews and create google doc w/ takeaways from each

## Attend first client performance call + take detailed notes
## Once done: they will then setup their first NEW actual client campaign
## Day 11-14: Ramp Up Client Load and Hit KPI Flow
Day 11 we assign the first 3-5 clients to them to officially start managing active clients

Day 14: check in call to audit changes made to those clients and do any necessary coaching

Gradually ramp the media buyer up from here to day 30 to managing 30-50 client campaigns

## The 3 Pillars of Media Buyer Success
Training a new media buyer is like a builder building a house. You wouldn't just throw them some wood and metal and wish them good luck. You need to provide a blueprint, the right tools, and a project manager to keep them on track. The same applies to your media buyers. There are three key pillars to their success:

## Show Them What Success Looks Like (Clarity)
They need to know exactly what's expected of them - their role, their KPIs, what "winning" looks like for them, for the clients, and for you.

For example: "Your mission is to manage 20-30 client campaigns, keep CPL below $12 and schedule rate above 10%, refresh ads every 3-4 days, and communicate proactively with CSMs when campaigns are out of KPI. You will do this by following our systems, using our content drive, and taking detailed notes on all updates."

## Give Them the Resources They Need to Win (Tools & Training)
This means access to all your systems (Monday.com, GHL, Fathom, Slack), all your SOPs, all relevant client info, and a structured training program - the bootcamp we're about to build.

## Give Them the SUPPORT They Need to Stay on Track (Guidance & Accountability)
Regular check-ins, 1-on-1s, team meetings, and clear access to you or a manager when they have questions or hit a wall.

I compare training team members to kinda like a builder building a house. We give them the resources to build the house, but we wouldn't just throw them some wood and metal and wish them good luck. We give them a BLUEPRINT (clarity), we make sure they have the right tools (resources), and have regular check-ins with the project manager (support...)

## The Media Buyer Training Bootcamp
The key thing that allows us to speed up this entire process is our Media Buyer bootcamp. The bootcamp is structured in folders, and each folder contains modules that the media buyer needs to complete.

They should complete one folder at a time - this way they're not getting overloaded with new information but not knowing how to implement it. Instead, we have them learn one specific thing related to the role, then we validate that they understand how it works, then we have them do it in real time.

Only once this is done do we move on to the next module they need to master or topic to cover.

With every position we start them off by going through the Entire Team Module which everyone goes through, this goes over the following:

## Platforms & Tools
There’s 3 key things you need when making bootcamps, you need 1. somewhere to house the content that your team can watch on 2. A tool to record the content 3. A tool to write out the written sop format, here’s my recommendations:

Skool: This will be where you house your videos and where your team can watch the bootcamp content

Loom: This is what you’ll use to record the videos, this integrates directly with skool so you can immediately upload a link

## Google Docs: Pretty self explanatory, it’s exactly what I'm using right now
The reason I'm a fan of these  tools over anything else is because for the hosting platform, I’ve tried to use Monday, Clickup, Kajabi - I just personally found that Skool is the most user friendly, now if you’re looking for an option that's free and similar to Skool, I’d recommend using GHL as they recently brought out an exact copy of skool that's included in GHL’s plan.

For recording you CAN manage videos on Wistia, Vimeo etc - Loom is just cheaper, easier to record, and will save you time downloading and uploading content to a video hosting platform. Just use loom it’ll make your life 10x easier.
