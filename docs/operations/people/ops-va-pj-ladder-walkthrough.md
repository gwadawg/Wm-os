# How this role grows

Draft for us to walk through together. This is what I am thinking. It is not the signed agreement yet. Read it, mark what is unclear, and tell me where you see it differently.

The path we are locking now goes through **R$10.000**. Anything above that is not written yet. We will design it later, when this seat is real. It is not a promise, and it is not off the table.

You are paid for the seat you can hold. From that day, you are already practicing the next seat with me, until you can do that work without me in it. The seat you are paid for still has to hold while you practice the next one.

A level turns on when the same standard has shown up on **3 to 5** real cases, and I would hand you the next one without briefing it. Three is the minimum. Five is the most you need to show. Hitting five does not turn the level on if I would still have to walk you through the next one.

You are paid for the calendar month, the **1st through the last day**. We pay that month on the **9th** of the next month. October 1–31 is paid on November 9. A new level starts on the 1st of the next month after we both initial the checklist. You invoice as PJ and handle your own taxes. Send the invoice by the 5th.

| Level | Monthly | What this seat is |
|---|---|---|
| Training | R$4.000 | Month 1. You learn the tools beside me. |
| Holding | R$5.000 | You are working, and I am still carrying it. |
| L2 | R$6.000 | You are the VA. Fundamentals, with me guiding. |
| L3 | R$7.000 | That same seat, without me. |
| L4 | R$8.000 | You fix snapshot bugs, improve workflows, and take on internal builds. |
| L5 | R$10.000 | You spec and ship systems. I review once. |

## Training — R$4.000

Month 1 only. Access, shadowing, and supervised reps on real tickets and a live client setup. There is no automatic raise at day 30.

By the end of the month you can find a client in ClickUp, follow the tech setup checklist, and write a ticket note I can read.

## Holding — R$5.000

This is the number we already talked about for the seat. It runs after month 1 until L2 is signed. You are doing the work, and I am still diagnosing it, rewriting it, or handing it back to you.

## L2 — R$6.000. You are the VA

You own the fundamentals. You are still learning how the company flows. I teach, and I review before work goes out.

- You close tickets in GHL, Hot Prospector, and Closebot. The note says what broke, what you changed, and where. I still spot-check.
- You run a client’s A2P and tech setup. I review before tech QA is submitted. You do not hand me the client to finish.
- You take team requests that are those systems: a form, a calendar, routing, a pixel, a booking path, a phone, a Closebot flow. A fire, or a client waiting to launch, comes first.
- You are already practicing the L3 work with me: a full onboarding, Closebot and the AI agent, and writing an SOP.

The media buyer keeps funnels, ads, strategy, budgets, and client KPI calls.

L2 is signed when you are in that guided seat. Doing it without me is L3, not L2.

## L3 — R$7.000. The seat stands without me

You enter when all of this is already true:

- **3 to 5** clients have gone through tech setup to tech QA, with you as the owner. You know the stages from payment to the account going live. Client success moves the stage. You do not.
- You run Closebot bugs and fix the AI agent. You fix what you can. When something has to go higher, you send it up with the cause, what you already tried, and what you recommend. I am not the first stop.
- You have written an SOP from work you have already done, and you have updated a broken process so the next run follows the new version.

While you are here, you get more of the build. You help on automations and on other advanced tasks, with me still available. The aim is clarity: how the automations actually fire, and every related skill you can pick up. You do not have to be strong at building automations yet to leave this level.

You leave for L4 when all three are true:

- On **3 to 5** live clients you can show the snapshot that account came from, the fields a workflow reads, and which of those values came from the client.
- You can walk how the company connects, systems-wise: onboarding, client success, media buying, and the call center, and where those meet in Mr. Waiz, ClickUp, Make, GHL, Closebot, and Hot Prospector.
- For **3 to 5** straight weeks I did not have to fix a broken system. If I have to step in and fix one, that stretch starts over.

## L4 — R$8.000. You work inside the snapshots

Everything from L3 stays yours. Onboardings, Closebot, the AI agent, SOPs, and broken systems do not come back to me.

You go deep on client bugs inside the snapshots. **3 to 5** on real clients. You find the workflow, the value, or the field that is wrong, you fix it, and it stays fixed.

You also improve workflows that already exist, including when nothing is on fire. **3 to 5** of those. A manual step removed, a failure that kept coming back stopped, or a workflow changed so it matches how that client’s information actually arrives. I did not design the fix.

If the edit is only that client’s account, you ship it. If the edit changes the master snapshot, the template every client is built from, you show me once before it publishes. You still do the work. I see it because it lands on every account.

You also take larger development tasks in our internal software, while the back end keeps running. This is where you learn that software in earnest. Shipping a whole system on one light review is the next level.

## L5 — R$10.000. You ship systems

**3 to 5** systems you specced, built, and shipped. Each one got a single review from me. The notes were small: a label, a field, an edge case. Then it went live. If my review changes the design, that one does not count. One small automation does not count. A system has more than one step and a clear done state.

The L4 work stays yours while you build.

If we open another VA seat, you run that person: the assignments, the review, and the quality. You can propose the hire. I still approve the seat and the pay. You do not have to wait for a hire to reach R$10.000.

## What stays with me

At every level I keep the offer, pricing, and anything that changes what we promised a client. I keep spend, new headcount, and any access that moves money. I see a master snapshot change once before it goes out.

## When the week is full

1. Something that stops leads or appointments.
2. A client in tech setup who is waiting to launch.
3. System tasks already assigned.
4. Team requests on those systems.
5. New builds.

## How we will work the ladder

- You can ask for a review any week you think you have the evidence. I also look at day 30, then monthly.
- We both initial the checklist. Pay changes the following month.
- If a seat you already hold comes back to me for a full month, the level steps down the month after. The month already underway is paid at the level we signed.
- A blocker is written within 30 minutes. A deadline is flagged before it slips. A problem comes with three options and one recommendation. The end-of-day note is in.

## Walk through this with me

- Where does this match how you want to grow, and where does it not?
- After month 1, which seat do you think you will actually be in?
- Which bar is unclear?
- What are your core hours, and your timezone?
